# T0.43 — Deploy check falls back to the vercel.app address
_2026-09-27T21:27:32Z_

aeni.ma stopped resolving on 2026-09-24 — the `.ma` registry holds no delegation for it — and the
deploy check, which asked the domain alone, has answered unknown ever since while merges kept
landing. It now asks the addresses `deploy` in `.claude/board.json` names, in order: the domain,
then the deployment's production alias on Vercel, `aenima-ae-nima.vercel.app`. A name that does not
resolve is skipped and never counted, and the outcome is the first address's that answers at all.
Guidelines v1.30 says so in §5's step-0 row and *The deploy check*.

The ticket's premise held only for a browser signed in to Vercel. From outside, every
`*.vercel.app` address of the project answers every path with a 302 to Vercel's login at
vercel.com, setting `_vercel_sso_nonce` — which, read as an answer, is `/sign-in` answering 302
rather than 200: down, and the first run after the merge would have reverted a healthy merge, and
every one after it while the domain stayed out. So Vercel's login is read as Vercel answering in the
deployment's place — skipped and named, never down — known by its nonce at any status or a redirect
to vercel.com. `VERCEL_AUTOMATION_BYPASS_SECRET` in `.env.local`, Vercel's Protection Bypass for
Automation, lets the check through; it is sent to `vercel.app` hosts alone, since the undelegated
domain could be registered by anyone. Until a human creates it, or lifts the login from production,
the check answers unknown and says why, address by address. The first review pass marked the one-
shape wall detection a Must on the chance Vercel serves a 401 instead; a measurement with Node's
`fetch` settled that it serves the 302 today, and the nonce now covers both.
