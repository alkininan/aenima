## How to read this document

This is the design half of the pair. `product-spec.md` says what the product does; this document says what it looks like and how it behaves, to the pixel and the millisecond, so that a build never has to invent a value. Board tickets cite both by section — `§8.18` is the menu, `§7 Hit areas` a paragraph — and their tests cite §17 by check.

Everything stated here is law. "May" marks a permission, "v1.1" a deferral, and §16 lists what is decided not to exist. Where two rules seem to disagree, the order is §0's laws, then the token blocks (§2, §3, §5, §6), then the component rule, then anything else; a disagreement that survives that order is a defect in this document — file a Decision and stop, never pick one. A value the build needs and cannot find here is the same case. A bare § is this document; a reference into the other half of the pair is written `product-spec §n`.

The platform baseline is Baseline Newly available at this version's date: every feature relied on is supported, without a partial implementation, in the current stable release of every core browser — Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari on desktop, and Chrome, Firefox and Safari on mobile — and a feature that reached that point recently is named as such. One API gates with no fallback: Popover (every panel, toast and tooltip is one; Newly available since January 2025). Anchor positioning and view transitions enhance and never gate (§6) — same-document view transitions reached Newly available in October 2025, and anchor positioning ships in every core browser but is used only in the parts that agree across engines (§6) — the disclosure works with JavaScript off (§8.25), and the glass recipe ships with its `-webkit-` twin and carries its own fallback (§5).

This document changes only in the repository, by a ticket that names its replacements per section, bumps the version and adds one line to the header comment; the copy in project knowledge is a mirror, refreshed afterwards. §17 is what every ticket citing this document is checked against.

## 0. Design language

**One line:** a liquid-glass instrument with a retro terminal heart and tactile controls — optimistic tech, dark, calm, precise.

Three threads run through every component:

1. **Glass** — dark translucent surfaces with real blur and a specular top edge. Modern, aero, expensive.
2. **Terminal** — data speaks monospace: readouts, IDs, timestamps, meters, micro-labels in JetBrains Mono uppercase. The retro heart.
3. **Tactile** — controls physically press: down instantly, release with a spring. Pill shapes, light fills with dark labels (the retro grammar), visible click feedback.

Laws (override any component default):

1. **Calm authority.** No alarm styling. Meters and gaps never render in Danger red; risk is Warning-toned; idle work dims; nothing blinks.
2. **Danger is reserved** for destructive actions, validation errors, and diff deletions. Never for scores, gaps, or overdue states.
3. **One accent leads.** Aqua is the only brand accent in the chrome. Violet appears only on agent output. Everything else is neutral until it means something.
4. **Violet means the machine.** Anything the agent drafted or proposes carries Agent/Violet until a human confirms it; confirmed content drops the violet.
5. **Glass has an edge.** Every glass surface and primary control carries a 1px inner top highlight — the specular line is the signature.
6. **Data speaks mono.** Numbers, IDs, timestamps, states: JetBrains Mono. Prose speaks DM Sans. Titles speak Space Grotesk. Two sanctioned exceptions, both in tabular figures: a count standing as a headline speaks Space Grotesk (display-num), and digits a person types speak DM Sans (special-otp, input values).
7. **Dim, don't disable.** Idle/parked items dim — text steps to `--n-secondary`, the non-text parts to reduced opacity (§2) — and stay fully interactive.
8. **One term per concept** in copy; sentence case; no exclamation marks.
9. **Texture is rationed.** The dot grid appears only on decorative surfaces (login, empty states, hero) — never behind working content.
10. **Glass is the navigation layer, never the content layer.** Panels, modals, sheets, sticky bars and toasts float above content and carry the glass recipe (§5), and the pipeline strip — chrome that sits in the flow of the page — carries it too; lists, rows, cards and anything scrollable do not. Glass on content muddies hierarchy — repeated across a list it reads as a stack of translucent rectangles rather than as one surface — and the hierarchy it was reached for has to come from spacing, typography and density instead.
11. **The control becomes the surface.** A trigger that opens a panel expands into it — one material, the radius interpolating from pill to panel — rather than spawning a rectangle beside itself. Menus, selects, comboboxes, filter chips and popovers morph (§6); modals, sheets, tooltips and disclosures do not, because each already has an origin of its own — a scrim, an edge, a delay, the flow of the page — and a second one would say nothing.

## 1. Brand mark

The identity is the **Æ ligature** — two letters fused into one glyph, which is what the product does to spec and design.

**Working logo (flat Æ).** `ae-mark.svg` (vectorized, final). Geometric monogram: uniform stroke ≈ cap-height ÷ 8, gently rounded A apex, flat vertical arm terminals, equal-length E arms, the A's right side shared with the E's spine. Default rendering **#E0E5EB on dark**; #FFFFFF for emphasis; #0E0F11 on light surfaces — that last value is brand ink for a light background, not a reference to the app background, and it does not follow `--bg-base`. In product the mark renders via `currentColor` — the SVG's baked-in fill is overridden so tokens decide, and the component never carries a hardcoded hex. Always flat, one color — never gradients, never effects on the working mark.

**Sizes.** Sidebar 24px · favicon 16px (`ae-favicon.svg` — heavier variant: stroke ≈ cap-height ÷ 6, slightly shorter middle arm) · avatar: mark centered in a `var(--bg-base)` rounded square, corner radius 22% of the square — the avatar is the mark on the product's own ground, so it follows the ramp rather than pinning a hex that goes stale the next time the ramp moves · packet and doc headers 32px. Clearspace ≥ one stroke-width on all sides. Minimum 14px.

**Wordmark.** `aenima`, lowercase, DM Sans SemiBold, tracking 0, to the right of the mark at cap height, gap = one stroke-width. `AENIMA` extended caps = marketing/merch retro lockup only, never product chrome.

**Hero (glass Æ).** The liquid-glass render (refraction, aqua→blue→violet internal gradient): landing hero, login backdrop, onboarding splash, OG card. Never product chrome, never below 200px.

## 2. Color system

Dark-only. Every accent that carries a label has two tones: **fill** (surface color; label sits on it) and **bright** (text/icon/stroke on dark, ≥7:1 on Base). Softs are the bright tone at low alpha. Copy-paste tokens:

## 3. Typography

Three faces, all SIL Open Font License, all self-hosted woff2 (no CDN), `font-display: swap`, preload each face's Latin subset, and its Latin Extended subset as well when the locale is TR or NL, since those letters live there.

**What self-hosted means:** the browser never contacts a font CDN at runtime. Every `@font-face` resolves to a same-origin woff2 on our own domain. A build-time fetch that emits those files as static assets satisfies this (Next's `next/font/google` does exactly that), and so does vendoring the woff2 files into the repo — but only Space Grotesk may come from Google Fonts. Its builds of the other two fall short of this section: DM Sans 4.004 carries no `tnum` feature, so `tabular-nums` does nothing in it and law 6's typed-digits exception cannot render, and JetBrains Mono 2.211 lacks Ĳ ĳ and the ⌘ ⇧ glyphs the kbd hint draws (§8.15). DM Sans is vendored from its upstream repository (googlefonts/dm-fonts) and JetBrains Mono from its own at 2.305 or later, both loaded with `next/font/local`; C-39 checks every shipped face for the coverage below, and DM Sans and Space Grotesk for `tnum`. Latin Extended is required on all three faces — TR `ğĞşŞİı` and NL `ĳĲ` are non-negotiable coverage. Where a face ships as a variable font, one file per subset covers every weight in the scale below and no weight arrays are declared.

```css
--font-display: 'Space Grotesk', system-ui, sans-serif; /* titles — retro-tech voice */
--font-ui: 'DM Sans', system-ui, sans-serif;            /* prose + controls */
--font-mono: 'JetBrains Mono', ui-monospace, monospace; /* data, readouts, code */
```

Root 16px. Space Grotesk styles: letter-spacing −1%. Full Latin Extended: TR/NL covered on all three faces.

**A button label is a control, not content, and controls sit one step below content in the ramp.** 17 is reserved for what a person reads or wrote — input values (ui-input) and row titles (ui-headline) — so pairing a button's label to its field's value makes a full-width primary read as a heading.

Rules: max reading measure 68ch (doc reader). Links `--prime`, underline on hover only. All numerals in data contexts use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`. The mono-micro eyebrow replaces v1's DM Sans eyebrow — the terminal label is the retro signature, use it wherever a tiny section label appears.

**Truncation.** Prose wraps; everything else is one line — controls, chips, row cells, readouts, subtitles, breadcrumbs. A one-line string truncates with a trailing ellipsis, except strings whose tail disambiguates — file names, email addresses, IDs and version tuples — which truncate in the middle so both ends survive. Titles wrap to two lines and then truncate; a page title is one line. The `title` attribute is not the affordance: on pointer devices a truncated string shows its full text in the tooltip component (§8.14) on hover or focus; on touch it does not need to, because a truncated row leads to the page where that string is content, and content is never truncated.

## 4. Layout, z-order, chrome

- **Grid.** Sidebar 240 · content column max 1200, centred, gutters 24 (16 in hand chrome) · chat dock 380 · item page = content 1fr / chat 380 while the dock is docked. Root 16px and breakpoints in CSS px, so browser zoom degrades into the narrower mode rather than breaking the wider one. **A mode's content column includes its gutters; the content box is the column less two gutters, and every container rule measures the content box** — the row's line count (§8.27) among them.
- **Layout modes.** Four, decided by viewport width alone. Touch and pointer are decided separately (§7), so a narrow desktop window gets hand chrome with pointer rules, an 11-inch iPad in portrait gets hand chrome with touch rules, and a 13-inch iPad in landscape gets the sidebar with touch rules.

| Mode | Width | Chrome | Content column | Chat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wide | ≥1440 | sidebar | min(1200, vw − 620): 820 at 1440; 1200 with the dock closed; beyond 1820 the surplus is margin, split evenly around the column | docked, open by default |
| Standard | 1280–1439 | sidebar | vw − 240, less 380 while the dock is open: 660–819 open, 1040–1199 closed | docked; closed by default on lists, open by default on the item page |
| Desk | 1024–1279 | sidebar | vw − 240: 784–1039 | overlay drawer |
| Hand | <1024 | top bar | vw | overlay drawer down to the read-only line; the read-only full-height bottom sheet below it |

  The arithmetic is the reason for the cut-offs. A docked dock beside a 240 sidebar leaves 660 at 1280 and 404 at 1024: 660 holds the item page and the two-line row (§8.27), 404 holds nothing, so the dock docks at 1280 and floats below it. A 240 sidebar beside content at 768 leaves 528, which fits nothing on the row, so the sidebar yields to a top bar below 1024. **Below the read-only line the product is read-only** — the line is 768 on touch and 600 on pointer, because a 200% zoom on a 1280 laptop is 640 wide and is not a phone (§13) — every mutation absent from the page, not disabled (the list is under Top bar) — **except the auth flow**, which must be fully usable at 375: an invited member signs in on whatever they are holding, and any control on the path from invite link to landed session carries a <768 rule. Walkthrough answers and signatures are the first v1.1 mobile writes (§16). **What a mutation is:** a control that writes carries `data-writes`, and below the read-only line no component renders one outside the auth path — gap moves, park, confirm, redirect, decide later, the walkthrough answer, the drag handle, bulk selection, the sign block, the composer and every settings form. Sign out, Retry, Copy, filters, paging, Show more, the product switcher and Jump to latest do not write and remain; C-25 asserts on the attribute.
- **The 768 line.** A component breakpoint on the viewport, separate from the read-only line: below it modals and side sheets are bottom sheets (§8.21), the OTP steps down (§8.2), toasts span the width (§8.20), tabs, the pipeline strip and tables scroll rather than wrap (§8.19, §8.26, §8.22), the stepper drops its labels (§8.31) and a chart takes the full width (§9). It applies at the same width on pointer and touch. It equals the touch read-only line because phones are narrow, not by rule, so on pointer between 600 and 767 these components take their narrow form and keep their writes.
- **Height.** The supported floor is 600 tall; below it nothing is hidden, the page scrolls. Sticky bars keep their height at every height. Modals and bottom sheets are at most 90% of the viewport tall — the full-height transcript sheet of §8.21 excepted — and a side sheet is the full height; all three scroll inside themselves and keep their footer or header pinned. The auth step keeps the Æ mark and drops its top spacing to 16 below 600 tall, so a landscape phone still sees a step whole. The dock's transcript scrolls inside the dock and its composer is pinned.
- **Density.** List rows 56 in a content box of 760 or more, 72 as two lines below it (§8.27) · table rows 44 · menu and option rows 36 on pointer, 44 on touch · hit areas ≥40 on pointer devices, ≥44 on touch, menu and option rows excepted on pointer (§7 Hit areas).
- **Sidebar (wide, standard, desk).** `--bg-base`; **a column of three zones — lockup, nav, account.** Lockup top: Æ mark 24px + `aenima` wordmark, one link to the dashboard; product switcher directly beneath it = avatar 40 + display-md, chevron `nav-arrow-down` 20, a menu trigger (§8.18) whose rows are the products; nav items 40 on pointer and 44 on touch (icon 20 + ui-body) — flush rows cannot extend a hit area, so on touch they grow for real, as menu rows do (§7 Hit areas) — active = `--prime-soft` pill + `--n-primary`. **Geometry:** the sidebar pads 16 inline; the lockup and the switcher are 56 rows, the bar height; the nav begins 16 below the switcher, its items flush with 12 inline padding and 8 from icon to label (§8.2's gap); the account slot is a 56 row. The nav's four glyphs, from Iconoir (§8.11): Dashboard `dashboard-dots` · Triage `mail-in` · Graveyard `archive` · Settings `settings`; Sign out is `log-out` wherever it appears. **The account slot is pinned to the bottom:** avatar 32 + the signed-in address in ui-body, truncating; it is a menu trigger (§8.18) whose rows are "Keyboard shortcuts" (`key-command`, §8.38 — always on pointer devices, and on touch once the modality script has recorded keyboard input, §8.15) and "Sign out" (`log-out`). Sign out is a plain row and takes no confirm: ending a session destroys nothing that was saved, and a composer draft is a draft. Identity is something you check and change, not something you navigate between, and the top-left position belongs to the things that move you around — the switcher stays at the top because it filters the list rather than identifying the person. There is no collapse control on the sidebar; below 1024 the top bar replaces it, which is a breakpoint rather than a control (§16). The chat dock is what collapses.
- **Top bar (hand).** 56h plus `env(safe-area-inset-top)`, sticky, glass recipe with `--scrim-top`: the Æ mark 24 at the left, one link to the dashboard as in the sidebar's lockup, the product switcher beside it (avatar 32, name display-md, truncating), and at the right a `chat-bubble` IconButton and a **menu IconButton** (`menu`), both Neutral 34, the menu one morphing (§6) into the nav menu — Dashboard, Triage, Graveyard, Settings, a separator, the account row — static, not a `menuitem`, standing where a section title would (avatar 24, address truncating) — then "Keyboard shortcuts" (on pointer devices, and on touch once keyboard input has been recorded, §8.15) and Sign out beneath it. One mechanism, no bottom tab bar. The page title steps down to display-lg; the topbar's primary action becomes an IconButton when it has an icon and a sm button when it does not. Mechanics: `viewport-fit=cover` with safe-area insets on every sticky bar, sheet and toast; no input font below 16 at any width, so Safari never zooms a field; `touch-action: manipulation` on controls; `-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent`, since press physics are the tap feedback; a focused field scrolls into view above the software keyboard. **Below the read-only line the mutations are absent:** every `data-writes` control — gap moves, park, confirm, overflow menus, bulk selection, the sign block and the composer among them — is not rendered, so nothing invites a tap it will refuse.
- **Chrome renders before data.** The sidebar, the top bar, the page topbar and the dock are drawn from the route alone, before any request returns, and chrome is never disabled while content loads — the person can always navigate, search and open the chat, whatever the page is still fetching. The two data slots inside chrome — the page topbar's title where it is an item's name, and its freshness readout — carry a skeleton of their own size until their data arrives, and the dock's transcript is content and loads per §10; nothing else in chrome ever skeletons. This is the PlayStation 5's one unarguable lesson: its Control Center answers a single press at once, over a game that is still loading, and that immediacy is most of what the interface is praised for.
- **Chat placement.** The dock is aenima's Control Center: one key from anywhere (`Cmd/Ctrl+K`), over the work rather than instead of it, the work still running underneath, and it puts the person back where they were when it closes. *Docked* (wide, standard): a 380 column at the right edge that pushes the content column, opening and closing in one frame (§6 — animating the column would re-wrap every row each frame). It closes only by the page topbar's `chat-bubble` IconButton: `Cmd/Ctrl+K` opens it and never closes it, and it is not in the Esc stack (§11). *Overlay drawer* (desk, and hand down to the read-only line): 380 wide from the right edge at rung 200, sliding its own width over `--t-med` and leaving over `--t-fast`, with no scrim — clicks on the content behind it go through, since the point of a drawer over a document is to read both — opened by the `chat-bubble` IconButton in the page topbar or the hand top bar, or by `Cmd/Ctrl+K`; closed by its own close IconButton (`xmark`), Esc, or `Cmd/Ctrl+K` from the composer. As an overlay the dock keeps `--bg-base` — a transcript is content, and §0 law 10 keeps glass off content — and takes `--shadow-modal`, since it now floats. *Bottom sheet* (below the read-only line): the full-height sheet of §8.21, read-only, with the subtitle "Reply from a desktop" standing where the composer would be. **Defaults and memory:** the dock is open by default everywhere in wide mode; in standard mode closed by default on lists and open by default on the item page, because the chat is the work there; closed everywhere in desk and hand. The defaults apply until the person toggles the dock, and from then the choice holds for the session, separately for list pages and the item page. The dock survives route changes — transcript, draft, scroll position and a stream in flight carry across — and remembers which of its controls had focus when it closed: reopening by the toggle lands focus there, or on the composer when nothing is remembered; `Cmd/Ctrl+K` always lands on the composer where there is one (§8.33), because the key is the command palette. (The PS5's Switcher, which closes what it claims to switch between, is the failure this rule exists to avoid.)
- **Topbar per page.** 56 tall — the hand bar's and the selection bar's height — growing by 30 when it carries a subtitle, inline padding the mode's gutter: display-xl title (display-lg in hand chrome) + mono-readout freshness + at most one primary action, and in the sidebar modes a `chat-bubble` IconButton (Neutral 34, `aria-pressed` while the dock is open) at the trailing edge after the primary. In the sidebar modes the topbar is sticky, glass recipe + `--scrim-top`; in hand chrome only the app top bar sticks and the page topbar scrolls with the page — two stacked sticky bars would take a fifth of a 600-tall screen. **A sticky bar's scrim is its own non-interactive layer directly behind the bar,** the bar's width, running from the bar's outer edge to three-sevenths of the bar's height beyond its content-side edge — `calc(<bar height> * 3 / 7)`, 24 beyond a 56 bar, and the same fraction of their own height for the topbar with a subtitle and for the hand top bar with its safe-area inset — so the scrim's alpha where content meets the bar is 0.144 on every bar: content dims as it approaches the bar and as it passes under the glass, a margin on top of the §13 ratio the glass fill already holds.
- **Subtitle slot.** Any page or step title may carry one subtitle line beneath it: ui-body, `--n-secondary`, 8 below the title. Instructional copy ("We'll send a six-digit code") lives here, and only here — never in a field's helper line (§8.2). One line; it truncates rather than wraps on narrow widths — except where the subtitle carries an address the person must check: the OTP subtitle wraps to as many lines as the address needs and never truncates it, since touch has no tooltip and no page holds that string as content.
- **Z-ladder** (never improvise): content 0 · sticky bars 100 · chat dock 200 · dropdown/popover 300 · modal scrim+modal 400 · toast 500 · tooltip and the skip link (§11) 600. **The ladder governs painting only.** Dismissal is last-opened-wins: `Esc` closes the most recently opened layer regardless of its rung, so a select opened inside a modal takes the first `Esc` and the modal takes the second. Ranking dismissal by rung would strand the inner layer. **The ladder governs what is not in the browser's top layer.** Popovers and dialogs paint in the top layer in the order they opened, above every rung — which puts a menu above the modal it was opened from, the same answer the ladder gives. Toasts and tooltips are `popover="manual"` so they too live there. Top-layer order is opening order and a modal makes everything outside its own subtree inert, so a toast is not left to that order: **the toast host is a `popover="manual"` that is moved into the topmost open modal's subtree — as its last child, and otherwise the last child of `body` — and re-shown whenever a modal opens or closes,** so it paints above the scrim, stays operable and is last in document order for Tab; its clock carries over the move. A tooltip is created in the same place — inside the topmost open modal's subtree, otherwise in `body` — so one raised over a modal's control can be hovered (§8.14). The rung numbers remain the tie-break for everything outside the top layer — sticky bars, the selection bar, the dock.
- **Overlay interior.** Modals, sheets, and toasts pad 20; stacked content inside them gaps on the 8-grid. Panels (menu, select, combobox, popover) originate on their trigger and carry no gap — the trigger sits inside the panel's footprint for as long as it is open (§6 Morph).
- **Scrollbars.** 8px thin; thumb `--surface-2`, hover `--glass-border`; track transparent. Mechanism: `scrollbar-color` and `scrollbar-width: thin` inside `@supports not selector(::-webkit-scrollbar)`, and the 8px thumb with its `--glass-border` hover through `::-webkit-scrollbar` elsewhere — an engine that honours the standard properties discards every `::-webkit-scrollbar` rule on the same element, so the two must never meet. Firefox gets no hover colour. The gate is Firefox's answer to `selector(::-webkit-scrollbar)`, which has changed across its releases (153 said yes, 156 says no); a Firefox that says yes gets its default scrollbar, which is acceptable, and C-50 watches it.
- **Text selection.** `::selection { background: var(--prime-soft); color: var(--n-primary); }`
- **Border widths.** 1px everywhere; 2px reserved for meaning: the focus ring, the agent violet border, bucket accents, the tab underline, the drop target, the walkthrough highlight, the avatar-group ring, the stream caret and a chart's bar divider (§9).

## 5. Shape, glass, elevation

- **Radius tokens.**
```css
--r-pill: 999px; /* buttons, inputs, chips, toggle, avatars */
--r-lg: 24px;    /* sheets */
--r-md: 20px;    /* modals */
--r-sm: 16px;    /* cards, message bubbles */
--r-panel: 12px; /* menu, select, combobox and popover panels, toasts (renamed in v2.19: its old name sat one character from --r-sm) */
--r-xs: 8px;     /* tooltips, code blocks, kbd hints */
```
- **Proportional rule:** multi-line pills step down — a control taller than 72 uses radius = height ÷ 4, rounded to a whole pixel, up at .5 (Sociera's Tall input: 104h → r26; the composer at three lines, 92h → r23). **Nested rule:** an inner surface flush inside a rounded container takes the container's radius minus the container's padding, never its own token — a pill segment inside a `--r-md` bar with 6 padding is r14, not `--r-pill`. An inner radius larger than the space allows visibly breaks the outer corner, which is the one thing a rounded container cannot survive. Where the container's padding is at least its radius the corners no longer interact and the inner surface keeps its own token — a card at 20 inside a `--r-md` modal is `--r-sm`, not r0. **Never a square corner on a free edge:** a corner is square only where a surface is flush to the viewport's edge (a sheet's far edge, a full-width bar, the dock) or to a sibling in its own group (the rows between a ledger's first and last).
- **Glass recipe** (menu, select, combobox and popover panels, modals, sheets, sticky bars, toasts, the pipeline strip):
```css
background: var(--glass-fill);
-webkit-backdrop-filter: blur(var(--glass-blur)); /* the prefixed twin first, for the WebKit builds that still need it */
backdrop-filter: blur(var(--glass-blur));
border: 1px solid var(--glass-border);
box-shadow: inset 0 1px 0 var(--edge-highlight); /* the specular edge — mandatory */
```
**Blur applies only where page content can pass beneath a surface unscrimmed:** sticky bars, panels, toasts. Surfaces over `--bg-scrim` — modals, side sheets, bottom sheets — and in-flow glass (the pipeline strip) take the recipe without its two `backdrop-filter` lines: the scrim passes a fifth of the page and the glass fill 15% of that, so a blur behind a modal has nothing to blur, and the largest blurred areas in the product would otherwise be sheets on phones. A pinned header or footer inside a modal or sheet carries no material of its own — glass on glass. No-`backdrop-filter` fallback: solid `--glass-fallback`, keep border + edge. A tooltip stays solid `--surface-2`: it is too small for blur to read as anything but noise, and it is the one floating layer that is not navigation.
- **Elevation = surface step + border, not shadows.** Shadows only for truly floating layers: panels, toasts and a lifted row `--shadow-float` · modals, sheets and the overlay drawer `--shadow-modal`. Everything else flat.
- **Cards.** `--surface-1`, `--r-sm`, padding 20 when the card stands on the page by itself (proposal, triage, walkthrough, packet and gap cards) and 16 when it sits inside another surface (an evidence card under a check), optional `--glass-border`; cards also carry the inset edge highlight in `--edge-highlight-card` — quieter than glass.

## 6. Motion & tactility

```css
--t-fast: 120ms; --t-med: 200ms; --t-slow: 320ms; --ease: cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1);
/* the named long durations — decorative, one each */
--t-sweep: 700ms;     /* the primary's hover sweep (§8.1) */
--t-shimmer: 600ms;   /* the meter's one-time shimmer (§8.23) */
--t-skeleton: 1.2s;   /* one pass of the skeleton highlight */
--t-spin: 800ms;      /* one turn of the spinner (§8.17) */
/* delays and holds */
--delay-tooltip: 500ms; /* hover to tooltip (§8.14) */
--delay-working: 300ms; /* send to working line (§8.34) */
--hold-lift: 300ms;     /* touch hold to lift a drag handle (§8.28) */
```

Every duration the stylesheet uses is one of the tokens above. The timers that live in script rather than CSS — the 1.5s validation pause (§8.2), the 1.5s copy tick (§8.16), the 5s and 8s toast clocks (§8.20), the 60s resend cooldown (§8.4), the 0.5 px/ms sheet dismiss velocity (§8.21) — live in one constants module, `motion.ts`, that C-05 reads beside `globals.css`; no component carries a number of its own.

- **Which duration.** Hover and overlay fades: `--t-fast`. Press release, panels, drawers, sheets, modals and toasts: `--t-med`. Meter width: `--t-slow`. Anything not given a curve eases on `--ease`.
- **Responsiveness budget.** A control answers in the frame it is pressed in — the press physics are 0ms in for this reason — and a panel is showing, with focus placed, on the next frame, its morph running from there. A route change paints its chrome and the content's skeleton within 100ms of the click and its content within 1s; past 1s the skeleton stays until the request answers, when content or §10's error line replaces it — never a spinner page. Measured as Interaction to Next Paint on the kitchen-sink route in the browser check, the 75th percentile is at or under 200ms — web.dev's "good" line; C-45 says how the browser check drives and reads it. Motion that would cost a frame is cut, not slowed: this is why blur is rationed (§5), why layout never animates, and why the morph opts the root out. The PS5's interface is described first as fast; nothing else it does would survive being slow.
- **Every exit runs one step faster than its entrance, on `--ease`:** a `--t-slow` entrance leaves over `--t-med`, a `--t-med` entrance over `--t-fast` — the morphed panel excepted, which leaves in one frame (Morph, below). Exits never spring. A top-layer element leaves the top layer the moment it is hidden, so the code applies the leaving state, waits for `transitionend`, then hides — the `overlay` property that would hold it there is not in the baseline, and neither is transitioning `display` with `allow-discrete`, which Firefox does not do.
- **Entrances.** A modal fades in with its scrim and rises 8 over `--t-med`; a toast does the same. A bottom sheet rises its own height, and a side sheet or the overlay drawer slides its own width, over `--t-med`. Entrances use `@starting-style`.
- **Interruptibility.** Every open and close except the morph is a CSS transition on `transform` and `opacity`, so a reversal mid-flight starts from where the surface is. The morph cannot reverse: closing during it ends it at once — every close path, the popover's `toggle` to closed included, calls `skipTransition()` on a running morph.
- **No layout animates.** The docked dock opens and closes in one frame — animating the content column would re-wrap every row each frame and flip rows between 56 and 72 mid-flight (§8.27). The tab underline moves in one frame and tab labels fade colour over `--t-fast` (§8.19). Lists never stagger and rows never animate into a new order: data arrives whole. Follow-scroll, scroll-into-view and composer growth are instant — no programmatic scroll is ever smoothed. Content replaces its skeleton in place (§10). Route changes are instant (§8.3 — the sidebar and the dock persist across them and carry the continuity).
- **Press physics (the retro click — applies to all buttons, chips-with-actions, toggle, checkbox):** on `:active`, instantly (0ms in): `transform: translateY(1px) scale(0.985)`, edge highlight off, `box-shadow: var(--press-shadow)`. On release: spring back over `--t-med` with `--ease-spring`, which overshoots the rest position by a tenth of the travel before settling — the curve peaks at 1.10, and at 1px of travel a tenth of it is a tenth of a pixel, which is why the spring is safe here and nowhere larger. The press must feel immediate; only the release animates, and a re-press during the release starts from wherever the control is. **The gloss press darkens rather than lightens:** `color-mix(in srgb, var(--bg-base) 12%, transparent)` replaces `--press-overlay` on the primary, because a white overlay on a lit surface reads as a highlight, not a press.
- **Morph (the control becomes the surface).** When a trigger opens a menu, select, combobox, filter chip or popover, the panel does not appear beside it: the trigger's box grows into the panel's box, one material throughout, the corner radius interpolating from half the trigger's height to `--r-panel`. Open over `--t-med` on `--ease` — `--ease-spring` belongs to the press, where a tenth of the travel is a tenth of a pixel; on a select growing from 48 to 320 the same overshoot would bounce the panel's bottom edge by 27, which §0 law 1 forbids. **Close is instant on every path** — light dismiss, Esc, Tab and selection alike: the panel leaves in one frame and the trigger returns. The platform hides a light-dismissed popover before any code can wrap it (`beforetoggle` cannot be cancelled on close), so a close morph would run on some paths and not on others, and one close path is worth more than a return flow. **The container morphs; the contents fade.** The transition name sits on the panel's material layer only; the contents carry a second name and fade in over the last `--t-fast` of the open. Naming the contents warps them — a 34 pill becoming a 240×200 panel stretches every glyph between — and text is the worst case. The trigger is hidden by `opacity: 0` and `pointer-events: none` for the panel's lifetime, its box reserved, and returns with focus on close — never by `visibility`, because in Chromium an anchor hidden that way stops its anchor-positioned panel taking pointer events under the default `position-visibility`, which the mechanism below never sets.
  **Mechanism, so nobody re-derives it.** The panel is a `popover` — top layer, light dismiss and `Esc` for free, nested popovers closing innermost-first, which is §4's last-opened-wins. Placement is CSS anchor positioning inside `@supports (anchor-name: --a)` — `anchor-name`, an explicit `position-anchor` and `anchor()` only, never `position-visibility`, whose values differ between engines — with a JS positioner writing `top`/`left` from the trigger's rect on open, scroll and resize where it is not supported; the two must be pixel-identical. **Placement rule:** a panel grows downward, and toward the side with more room, unless its own height does not fit below the trigger's top edge; then it grows upward. If it fits neither way it opens toward the larger side with max-height equal to that room less 8. The choice is made at open and held until close. The morph is a same-document View Transition on open only: `document.startViewTransition` wraps the `showPopover()` call; the trigger carries `view-transition-name: morph` in the old state and the panel's material layer carries it in the new. **The image-pair is the material.** `:root::view-transition-group(morph)` keeps the engine's own size-and-position animation and is given only `animation-duration: var(--t-med)`, `animation-timing-function: var(--ease)` and `backdrop-filter: none`; `:root::view-transition-image-pair(morph)`, which fills the group, carries the glass recipe with `box-sizing: border-box`, `--shadow-float`, and a `border-radius` keyframe from `--morph-r0` (half the trigger's height, set inline at open) to `--r-panel` on the same duration and curve with `animation-fill-mode: forwards`; `:root::view-transition-old(morph)` and `:root::view-transition-new(morph)` are `display: none`, so nothing is stretched and the border stays 1px throughout; `:root::view-transition-new(morph-content)` fades in on the delay above. Nothing is ever animated on the group: an author animation there replaces the engine's, and the box would jump to its final size on the first frame while only its corners moved. The blur sits on the image-pair because that is where the radius is — on the group it would blur a square box behind round corners — and the group is told `none` so the engine does not copy the panel's blur onto it as well. Every selector on these pseudo-elements starts at `:root` with no combinator before it, since they hang off the root element. The root opts out (`:root { view-transition-name: none }`) and `:root::view-transition` takes `pointer-events: none`, so the page beneath keeps rendering — a streaming transcript never stalls for a menu — and keeps taking clicks. One transition runs per document: a second open skips the first to its end state, and nothing else in the product calls `startViewTransition`. Names are set at open and cleared at `finished`, never left on elements between transitions — two live elements sharing a name abort every transition on the page. **Focus is placed by the code inside the update callback, straight after `showPopover()`** — on the panel's first item, which in a select is the selected option and in a combobox the input — never left to the transition and never deferred to `finished`, because an opacity-hidden trigger keeps focus and a keystroke in the gap would land on a trigger nobody can see; the new snapshot carries the ring from its first frame and it fades in with the contents. Where `startViewTransition` is absent, or under `prefers-reduced-motion`, the callback runs bare and the panel simply appears: v2.17's behaviour, so the morph is pure enhancement and can never be the reason a menu fails to open. Element-scoped view transitions would do the root opt-out natively; they are in one engine today, so the document does not name them — revisit when they reach the baseline.
  **What morphs and what does not.** Overflow menus, selects, comboboxes, filter chips, the hand-chrome nav menu, the avatar group and any popover morph from their trigger, anchored at the trigger's nearest corner and placed by the rule above. The chat dock and side sheets slide from their edge over `--t-med`; modals and bottom sheets rise behind a scrim; tooltips have a delay and no origin; disclosures open in the flow of the page. Each already has an origin, and a second one would say nothing. Any open panel closes, and reopens only by hand, when the viewport's *width* changes or the orientation does — its anchor may have moved — and when the docked dock opens or closes, which moves every anchor in the content column without a viewport change; a height-only change (a collapsing browser toolbar, the software keyboard) leaves it open and re-places it.
- **Focus-visible** (keyboard only): `outline: 2px solid var(--prime); outline-offset: 2px;` plus `box-shadow: var(--prime-glow)` — the aero glow lives on focus and on live dots, nowhere else. Pointer focus is not keyboard focus: clicking into a field swaps its border to `--prime` and nothing more. Ring and glow are keyboard affordances. The mechanism is input-modality tracking, not `:focus-visible` — browsers deliberately match `:focus-visible` on a clicked text input, since it is about to take keystrokes, so gating the ring on it reproduces the double stroke this rule removes. Modality is recorded on `<html>` by an inlined script and **starts unset**: a field the page focused already carries a caret, which is the affordance, so ringing it as well reproduces on load the same double stroke this rule removes from the mouse path. The ring arrives on the first Tab or focus key — the first moment there is anything true to say about which device is in use. `:focus-visible` remains correct for buttons and links, where a click does not match it.
- Meter fill animates width `--t-slow`; it animates only a change it witnesses, never from zero on load. Reaching handover-ready (§8.23) triggers a one-time `--t-shimmer` `--hero-gradient` shimmer sweep, once per page open.
- Skeleton shimmer: `--surface-2` base, moving highlight `--hover-overlay`, `--t-skeleton` linear loop. Geometry: a 200%-wide linear-gradient (transparent → highlight → transparent) traversing the element left to right, one pass per loop.
- Small motions, so none is invented: the stepper's connector fills in `--prime` over `--t-med` (§8.31) · a drag lift takes `--t-fast` in and settles over `--t-med` on `--ease-spring` (§8.28) · a set-aside card collapses by animating `grid-template-rows` from `1fr` to `0fr` over `--t-fast`, since height cannot transition to `auto` in every engine (§8.34) · the primary's sweep never runs on a disabled or loading primary (§8.1).
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: kill shimmer, press-translate and press-squish and the primary's hover sweep (keep the inner-shadow state change — it alone carries the press), meter animation, lift, the floating-label slide (§8.2 — the label snaps between positions), the morph (the panel appears in place) and every slide: drawers, sheets, modals and toasts appear in place, a released sheet returns without the spring, and the set-aside card collapses at once. Opacity fades, colour changes and the spinner remain.

## 7. Interaction states (any interactive element)

| State | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Hover | `--hover-overlay` + cursor pointer; on glass, border brightens to `--glass-border-hover`; primary adds a one-pass sweep (§8.1) |
| Active/pressed | press physics above + `--press-overlay` (the primary takes §6's `--bg-base` tint instead) — squish 0.985 + spring release |
| Focus-visible | prime ring + glow (keyboard only — pointer focus changes the border alone, per §6) |
| Selected | `--prime-soft` fill, `--n-primary` text |
| Disabled | `--n-disabled` text/icon, fills at 40% opacity, no hover, cursor default, edge highlight off (the resend cooldown keeps its label readable — §8.4) |
| Loading | element-level spinner (below) or skeleton; never full-page spinners |
| Drag | scale 1.02 + `--shadow-float` |
| Open (a trigger whose panel is open) | the trigger is hidden inside the panel's footprint (§6 Morph); focus lands on the panel's first item — a select's selected option, a combobox's input — and returns to the trigger on close |

**Hit areas.** The floor — 40 on pointer devices, 44 on touch (§4) — is a rule about what a finger or a pointer can hit, not about how large a control draws. A control whose box is smaller than the floor keeps its size and extends its hit area to the floor with a transparent `::before`, centred on the box, on whichever axis falls short: sm and md buttons, chips, tabs, the toggle, the checkbox and radio, the drag handle, the sortable-header chevron, the copy button. Where such controls sit closer together than the extension — a chip row — the hit areas overlap and the later element in document order takes the tap; a free-standing destructive control keeps 8 clear around its hit area, so nothing overlaps it. **Menu and option rows are their own hit areas:** 36 on pointer, flush to their neighbours and exempt from the 40 floor — rows stacked flush have nowhere to extend into; 44 on touch, where the row grows for real and the panel's height is what changes. A destructive row is the last in its panel, beneath a separator that carries 6 above and below it — that block is its clearance.

**Input and gestures — pointer and touch.** One table so no interaction is decided in a component. Pointer rules are gated by `@media (hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`; touch is everything else, at any width — an iPad in hand chrome is touch, a narrow desktop window in hand chrome is pointer.

| Input | Pointer | Touch |
|---|---|---|
| Hover | `--hover-overlay`, border brightening, the primary's sweep, the overflow trigger brightening, the drag handle and row checkbox appearing in their reserved slot, tooltips after `--delay-tooltip` | Does not exist. Nothing is revealed by hover alone: the overflow trigger, drag handle and row checkbox are always rendered on touch |
| Click / tap | Fires on release; press physics from down to up | The same. `touch-action: manipulation`, so no double-tap zoom on controls and no tap delay |

## 8. Components

Confirmed dimensions come from Sociera v2.0's real components (Figma node introspection); web-only components follow the same grammar. The blocks are numbered so a ticket can cite one — `§8.18` is the menu — in the order they were drawn; a new block joins at the end and nothing renumbers.

### 8.1 Buttons

All pills. Sizes: **sm** 28h, pad 5/10, gap 4, icon 18, ui-button-sm · **md** 34h, pad 7/14, gap 4, icon 20, ui-button · **lg** 48h, pad 14/20, gap 4, icon 24, ui-button. All `--r-pill`. **Height governs and the vertical padding derives from it**, per the option-row rule — each is (height − the label's line box) ÷ 2, so 28 − 18 gives 5, 34 − 20 gives 7, 48 − 20 gives 14. Retune a size by moving the height and recomputing the padding, never by moving the height to match a padding that has gone stale. Variants:
- **Primary:** `--grad-primary` fill, label `var(--bg-base)`, 1px `--edge-highlight` top specular. The light-fill/dark-label pairing is deliberate retro grammar. Hover lightens with `--hover-overlay` and runs a single left-to-right specular sweep — `--sweep`, `--t-sweep`, `--ease`, one pass per hover-in, none under `prefers-reduced-motion` and none on a disabled or loading primary. Press darkens with §6's `--bg-base` tint and applies the press physics. **The gloss is the primary's alone:** danger and every other variant stay flat.
- **Soft:** `--prime-soft` fill, `--prime` label.
- **Neutral:** `--surface-2` fill, `--n-primary` label. For chrome that must stay visible without reading as an accent.
- **Secondary:** transparent, 1px `--glass-border`, `--n-primary` label; hover brightens border.
- **Ghost:** text-only, `--n-secondary` → `--n-primary` on hover.
- **Danger:** `--danger-deep` fill, white label; destructive actions always get a confirm step.
States: hover overlay · press physics · loading (spinner replaces label, width locked) · disabled. **Icon buttons:** 28/34/48 square, icon 18/20/24, padding (box − icon) ÷ 2 = 5/7/12, same radius and variants.

### 8.2 Inputs

Field 48h, `--r-pill`, `--surface-1` fill overlaid with `--sheen` beneath the top specular, 1px `--glass-border`, pad 16 horizontal (height wins: the value centres vertically, per §8.5's height-wins rule), icon slots 24 leading/trailing, gap 8, ui-input text. 48 aligns fields with the lg button, so a field and its submit sit at one height. Focus: pointer → `--prime` border only; keyboard modality (§6) → border + ring + glow. Error: `--danger` border. **Error outranks focus:** a focused field in error keeps the `--danger` border and icon; keyboard modality still adds the `--prime` ring outside it, and pointer focus on an errored field shows only the caret and the floated label. Disabled: 40% opacity. **The leading icon takes the field's state colour** — `--prime` on focus, `--danger` on error, `--n-secondary` otherwise, `--n-disabled` when disabled. The icon is part of the field's state, not decoration beside it; a field that signals only at its border makes the user hunt for what changed. **No `backdrop-filter` on a field:** the sheen is a material, not a layer, and blur belongs to the glass recipe's surfaces alone (§5).
**Floating label.** The label is a real `<label>`, always in the DOM, never a placeholder. At rest (empty, unfocused) it renders inside the field where the value will sit — at input size, `--n-secondary` (AA), not placeholder tone. The at-rest label sits inside the pill's 1px border, so its rest offset is 9 (41 behind a leading icon): label and value paint at the same x, and the border is part of the geometry, not a rounding error. On focus or once filled, it floats to the label zone above the field: ui-label (§3), `--n-secondary`, 6 above the field, left-aligned 8 from the field's left edge, in a label zone with 8 side padding — the label hugs the container edge regardless of leading icons; only the value moves for an icon. **The label floats on `transform` alone:** at rest the label is its floated self — ui-label — scaled by 17/13 from its top-left and translated into the field, so it reads at input size; the float animates `transform` only, over `--t-fast` on `--ease`, never `font-size`, which would re-lay the text out on every frame. At rest it is therefore Medium, a touch heavier than a typed value, which at `--n-secondary` is imperceptible. `prefers-reduced-motion` snaps it. The label zone (24h = 18 line + 6 gap) is **always reserved**, so floating never shifts layout. A field shows one text, ever: its label. Format-hint placeholders are abolished — after the label floats, the field is empty with a caret. Nothing swaps, nothing appears. **Exempt from the floating label:** Search (the leading icon names it; placeholder "Search" allowed at rest) and the chat composer (the dock names it; placeholder "Message aenima", §12) — both are labelled by context, not by a `<label>` row. `--n-placeholder` exists only for these two.
**Helper line speaks only in states.** The helper slot (ui-footnote, 8 below the field) carries validation outcomes exclusively — error `--danger`, warning `--warning`, success `--success` — never instructions; instructional copy lives in the subtitle slot (§4). **A field's helper line carries only errors about that field's own value.** A failure belonging to a control elsewhere on the step surfaces at that control, never on the nearest field; **a request-level message** — a rate limit, a send that failed — renders in one slot under the step's last control, ui-footnote `--n-secondary`, centred, wrapping (§8.10's precedent). **A field reserves as many helper lines as its longest message needs at 343 wide in the longest locale** — one (18h) under an ordinary field, two (36h) under the OTP group, whose wrong-code string measures 305 at 343 and crosses onto a second line under §12's +30% reserve — so an error appearing never shifts layout, and a message never truncates. **Validation timing — flag slow, clear fast.** Before submit a field never errors while empty or under 3 characters, blur included: leaving an untouched field is not a mistake. While typing, validation waits for a 1.5s pause; blur validates immediately once the field carries ≥3 characters. Submit validates everything, empty fields too, and scrolls to the first error; a submit is never disabled for an incomplete form — pressing it is how the person learns what is missing. The moment input becomes valid, the error clears instantly — flagging waits, clearing never does.
**Autofill paint.** Browser autofill (Chrome's yellow/white flash) is overridden as law, not left to chance: `input:-webkit-autofill { -webkit-box-shadow: inset 0 0 0 1000px var(--surface-1); -webkit-text-fill-color: var(--n-primary); caret-color: var(--prime); transition: background-color 9999s; }` — the field must look identical filled by hand or by the browser.
**Textarea/composer:** starts as the 48h pill and grows with its content by the line — 22 a line on 13 of vertical padding, so 70 at two lines and 92 at three — taking §5's proportional radius from the third line (92h → r23) and stopping at eight lines, 202h, where it scrolls inside. **Search:** leading icon variant of the standard field. **Date field:** standard pill field + native browser picker (custom calendar is v1.1). **OTP:** 6 boxes, special-otp centered, filled box border `--prime` — a distinct component with its own geometry, deliberately exempt from the 48 field height. **Two sizes:** ≥768 **52×52**, gap 16 · <768 **44×44**, gap 8; both `--r-pill`, which clamps to half the box. Six 52s and five 16s span 392 and overflow a 375 phone; the small scale spans 304 and fits, and meets §4's 44 touch floor exactly. The label is `sr-only` at both sizes: the step title ("Enter your code") is the visible name, and a label row beneath it would say the same thing twice. Note for implementers: the focus ring (2px at 2px offset, §6) reaches 4 into the small gap of 8 — clearance is tight but rings never touch. **Mechanics:** one logical input behind six boxes, `inputmode="numeric"`, `autocomplete="one-time-code"`; a paste fills every box; Backspace steps back one box; the sixth digit submits as a pending move (§8.34), so the primary is a second path to the same submit; a wrong code keeps the digits, turns all six borders `--danger` and selects them, so the next keystroke replaces the code whole.

### 8.3 Multi-step flows

Sign-in, onboarding, ceremony steps. **Geometry:** the step is a centred column of 400 (§8.21's and §8.24's bound), the mode's gutters outside it; the Æ mark 32 (§1's header size) is centered above every step, 48 below the top of the page — 16 below 600 tall (§4 Height); 24 from the mark to the title block; 24 from the title block to the first label zone; field blocks stack with no further gap, since the reserved helper lines and the next label zone are the space; 8 from the primary to the tertiary. The step title is display-lg (§3) and its subtitle is the §4 subtitle slot. A step with no back button centers its title and subtitle beneath the mark. A step with a back button uses the header grammar instead: a row of back control then title, with the subtitle under the title — title and subtitle left-aligned to each other, never centered beside a back control. The back is an IconButton, neutral variant (visible `--surface-2` circular fill at rest — ghost disappears in a header), 48, `arrow-left` 24, gap 12 to the title block. Back always means "previous step" and replaces textual escape hatches that mean the same thing ("Use a different email" is the back button). Controls follow, then the primary lg filling the column; tertiary actions ("Send a new code") beneath it, centered, Neutral md, maximum one per step. **Back and the tertiary use the same variant:** they are the same tier of chrome on the same step and must read as one family. Ghost is wrong for a tertiary that can be disabled and counting down — it vanishes precisely when it has the most to say. The OTP group stays centered (six discrete boxes are a display, not a text field). Step changes are instant — no slide transitions in v1, and the same holds for every route change in the product (§6): a route cross-fade would be a document view transition, which is the morph's one slot and would stall a streaming dock.

### 8.4 Resend cooldown

A tertiary resend action ("Send a new code") is disabled for 60s after a code is sent and counts down in its own label — "Send a new code (0:47)" — returning to its normal label at zero. The cooldown starts when a code is sent, not when the resend is pressed: arriving at a code step means one has just gone out, so the resend opens already counting down. The provider's window belongs to the address, not to the control, and a resend that is live on arrival hands the user exactly the rate-limited tap the cooldown exists to prevent. The cooldown is the mechanism, not advice: without it a second tap hits the provider's rate limit and the failure lands on whatever field is on screen, which blames the user for a control the product left enabled. A control that cannot succeed for a reason the person cannot fix — a cooldown, a request in flight — is disabled, never merely apologised for. **While it counts down the control is disabled in behaviour** — no hover, no press, cursor default, fill at 40% — **but its label stays `--n-secondary`** and the time is a mono-readout span inside it, so the one changing thing on the step is readable and its width holds still: §13's disabled exemption is for controls that carry no information, and this one carries the only information on the step.

### 8.5 Select

Trigger = pill field with trailing chevron (`nav-arrow-down`, 20) — it inherits the full input grammar, 48h and floating label included. Panel: glass recipe, `--r-panel`, `--shadow-float`, 6 padding. It **morphs from the field** (§6): top-aligned with the field's top and the field's width, placed by §6's rule — downward when its own height fits below the field's top edge, upward otherwise, and toward the larger side with max-height equal to that room less 8 when it fits neither way — with the field hidden beneath it for the panel's lifetime. Options 36h (44h on touch), ui-body, pad 12 horizontal, radius 6 (§5's nested rule: 12 − 6); hover `--hover-overlay` (the panel is already `--glass-fill`, so `--surface-3` would be its own colour); selected `--prime-soft` + check icon 16, scrolled into view on open. **Height wins:** 36 comes from §4 density and governs the whole system; the label centres within it rather than adding vertical padding. At most 320 tall, its 6 padding included, the options scrolling inside; type-to-jump. `role="combobox"` on the trigger, `role="listbox"` on the panel: arrows move the active option, Enter selects, Esc closes without change, Tab selects the active option and closes, carrying focus onward (§11) — the native `<select>` contract, so nobody relearns it.

### 8.6 Combobox

For assigning a person, an opportunity, a Decider. The select grammar with a typeable row: **the trigger is a field-shaped button showing the chosen value** — 48h pill, floating label, the select's chevron. Click, Enter, Space, ArrowDown or the first typed character — carried in — morphs it into the select panel, whose first row is the real input: 48h, flush to the panel's top and sides — outside the panel's 6 padding, which begins beneath the input's separator — at the trigger's own text inset, so the value does not move on either axis; a 1px `--glass-border` separator beneath it, the options after. A hidden input can show neither text nor caret and drops focus the moment it hides, which is why the field lives inside the panel rather than beneath it. Typing filters the options as it goes; matches render the typed run in `--n-primary` and the rest in `--n-secondary`; no match renders the compact empty state (§10) "No matches" inside the panel; where creating is allowed, a last row "Create '{typed}'" in `--prime` sits beneath a separator. Arrows move the active option without leaving the input — the active option takes the hover fill and no ring, since focus stays in the input — Enter picks it, Esc restores the previous value and closes. A chosen value renders as the trigger's value, never as a chip inside it.

### 8.7 Checkbox & radio

20×20; checkbox radius 6, radio circle. Unchecked: `--surface-1` fill + `--glass-border`. Checked: `--prime` fill, `var(--bg-base)` check/dot — the radio dot is 8. Press physics apply. Label ui-body, gap 10, whole row clickable.

### 8.8 Toggle

56×28, `--r-pill`, 2px inset, thumb 24 circle. Off: `--surface-2` track, `--n-secondary` thumb. On: `--prime` track, `--n-white` thumb. Track/thumb transition `--t-fast`. Label ui-body, gap 10, whole row clickable — the checkbox's rule.

### 8.9 Chips & badges

Chip 24h, pad 4/10, gap 4, `--r-pill`, `--surface-2` fill, ui-caption; interactive chips get hover + press. (The vertical padding is derived, not free: ui-caption's 16px line box plus 4 above and below is exactly the 24 height; the 10 horizontal matches the sm button.) Type badge (Feature, Enhancement, Technical, Content, Experiment, Fix, Spike): outline chip, `--glass-border`, `--n-secondary` — types are informative, never colorful. **Not on the item row**, where the type drops its container so the only outlined thing in a row is a gap (§8.27). Gap chips: open Must = `--warning-soft` bg + `--warning` text · open Should = `--surface-2` + `--n-secondary` · accepted = `--surface-2` + `--n-secondary` + accepter name · excluded = transparent + `--n-disabled` outline + `--n-secondary` text (disabled tone on the text would hide the name, and the chip is information). **The chip's text carries priority and state, never colour alone** (§13): in the check list "Must · unclear", "Should · unclear"; on a row "Must · {check id}", the id in mono-readout; accepted "Must · {accepter}". Count badges: display-num in a `--surface-2` pill, 30h — the 22 line with 4 above and below — pad 10, the chip's; a badge stands only where a count is a headline beside a title, and the row's overflow count is a chip, not a badge (§8.27). **File chip:** chip + file-type icon 16 (`page`) + name (middle-truncate) + size in mono-readout.

### 8.10 Gap moves

product-spec §5's negotiation moves on a gap, wherever that gap is drawn — the gap card (§8.32) and the check list render the same control, never two.
- **The settled stamp** is ui-footnote `--n-secondary`: who accepted or excluded it, and their reason after an em dash. product-spec §1 law 7 makes a debt something a *named* person owns, so the name travels with the gap rather than living only on the card.
- **Accept** is a disclosure (§8.25) with a ui-footnote `--n-secondary` summary. Inside it, §8.2's field grammar unchanged — 48h pill, floating label, one reserved 18h helper line — and a **Primary md** submit.
- **Reopen** is a standing control beside the stamp, **Secondary sm and not Ghost**: a settled gap card is already dimmed (§8.32), and §8.3's own warning about a text-only control that "vanishes precisely when it has the most to say" is exactly this case. §0 law 7 dims it; nothing disables it.
- **Neither move takes a confirm step.** §0 law 2 reserves that for destructive actions, and accepting is not destructive, reopening is not either, and both have standing reversals.
- **Danger appears on exactly one thing here:** the reason field's own helper line on a validation error, which §0 law 2 names as one of its three sanctioned uses. Every other outcome a move can report is about the request rather than the field, and renders ui-footnote `--n-secondary` beside the form.

**A move's confirmation renders outside the disclosure its own outcome closes.** The accept form's `open` is decided by the outcome — a move that landed needs no form open behind it — so a message placed inside would be hidden by precisely the outcomes it exists to report. A disclosure decides whether the *form* is open and never whether a sentence is readable. This is a general rule about disclosures, not a note about one form: it shipped broken once, with a reversal's confirmation in the DOM inside a collapsed element, reaching nobody, on the one move product-spec §1 law 4 exists to make visible.

### 8.11 Icons

Packet signers, watchers. ### 8.14 Tooltip

`--surface-2`, `--r-xs`, ui-caption, pad 6/10, max-width 240, 8 from the trigger — below it and centred, flipping above when it would leave the viewport — no arrow, `--delay-tooltip` show delay, instant hide, z 600. It stays while the pointer is over the trigger, the tooltip or the 8 between them, and hides at once on leaving all three; it hides on Esc, which it takes first and which then closes nothing else (§11). Inside an open modal it is hosted in the modal's subtree (§4), so the pointer can cross onto it without the page's inertness hiding it. Tooltips are pointer-only: they never render on touch, so nothing may live only in one — the kbd hint is desktop-only by nature, and a truncated string's full text is reached through the page it leads to (§3).

### 8.15 Kbd hint

A shortcut rendered as a key: mono-readout, `--surface-2` fill, `--r-xs`, pad 2/6, 20h, `--n-secondary`, a 1px `--glass-border` on the bottom edge only — the cap of a key. Modifier glyphs follow the platform, `⌘` on macOS and `Ctrl` elsewhere, read from the user agent at load. The ⌘ and ⇧ glyphs come from the vendored JetBrains Mono (§3): the Google Fonts build has neither, and a fallback face would break the key's metrics. It appears trailing in menu rows, inside tooltips ("Focus chat ⌘K") and in the shortcut sheet (§8.38), and never on touch until the modality script has recorded keyboard input (§6, §7) — an iPad with a keyboard has every shortcut, a bare one has none.

### 8.16 Copy

Code blocks, prompt-pack panels and any identifier a person will paste — a version tuple, an incident id — carry a copy IconButton: Ghost, sm, `copy` icon, top-right of the block at 8 inset, `--n-secondary`, brightening on the block's hover and always rendered on touch. On press the icon swaps to `check` in `--success` for 1.5s and the tooltip component shows "Copied" at once — without its `--delay-tooltip` — hiding with the icon. No toast: a toast is for an action with consequences, and a copy has none.

### 8.17 Spinner

Ring 16/20/24, 2px stroke, `--prime` — `var(--bg-base)` on prime fills, `--n-white` on a Danger fill, `--agent` for agent work (§8.34) — `--t-spin` linear rotation. Element-level only.

### 8.18 Menus

Overflow menus (right-click stays the browser's, §16). Panel: glass recipe (§5 — it is navigation layer, and the morph needs trigger and panel to be one material), `--r-panel`, `--shadow-float`, 6 pad. It **morphs from its trigger** (§6), anchored at the trigger's nearest corner, min-width 200, max-width 280, placed by §6's rule. Rows 36h ui-body — 44h on touch — pad 12 horizontal, radius 6 (§5's nested rule), a 20 icon leading and an optional kbd hint trailing; hover `--hover-overlay` (§8.5's reason); destructive rows in `--danger` text, last in the panel, beneath a separator that carries 6 above and below it (§7 Hit areas); section titles mono-micro `--n-secondary`; separators 1px `--glass-border`. `role="menu"` with roving focus: opening from the keyboard focuses the first row, arrows wrap, Home and End jump, a typed letter jumps to the next row starting with it, Enter or Space activates, Esc closes and returns focus to the trigger, Tab closes and carries focus to the stop after the trigger, Shift+Tab to the one before (§11). **The overflow trigger on a row is always rendered** — `more-horiz`, `--n-secondary`, brightening on row hover — never hover-revealed, because touch has no hover.

### 8.19 Tabs

ui-subhead; inactive `--n-secondary`; active `--n-primary` + 2px `--prime` underline (radius 2); hover overlay on the tab's 36h box, the hit area extending to the floor per §7. The underline moves in one frame and the labels fade colour over `--t-fast` (§6). A `tablist` with roving focus: one Tab stop, arrows move and activate; a tab that is a route is a link with `aria-current`. Below 768 the tab row scrolls horizontally with the active tab scrolled into view; it never wraps.

### 8.20 Toasts

Bottom-centre, 24 above the viewport's bottom edge — 8 above a bottom-pinned bar when one shows — glass recipe, `--r-panel`, `--shadow-float`, max-width 400; leading dot `--success` for a move that landed and `--warning` for one that did not (§8.34) or for a sync retry, ui-body text and an optional action as a Neutral sm button ("Undo") — Neutral, not Soft, because an aqua label on `--prime-soft` falls under AA on glass with a bright fill beneath it (§13) — gap 8 between the three; never a red toast — errors surface inline. It enters as §6 says (fade and rise 8 over `--t-med`) and leaves over `--t-fast`. **Two clocks:** auto-dismiss 5s by default, 8s when the toast carries an undo action — undo needs reaction time (§12). Hover or focus pauses either. **One toast at a time, and the newest wins:** it replaces the one showing, undo or not — an undo toast is a shortcut to a reversal that also stands on the page (Reopen, the parked list, the set-aside line), so replacing it loses only the shortcut, whereas queuing it would leave an Undo on screen that means an earlier move than the one just made. Every move that offers an undo toast has such a standing reversal; a move that has none is pending, not optimistic (§8.34). A failure dismisses its own move's undo at once. While an undo shows, `Cmd/Ctrl+Z` triggers it when focus is not in a text field. z 500; its host is the `popover="manual"` of §4's ladder, moved into the open modal's subtree so it stays operable. Below 768 a toast spans the width minus 16 gutters and sits 16 above `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` — still 8 above a bottom-pinned bar when one shows.

### 8.21 Modals & sheets

Scrim `--bg-scrim`. Modal: glass recipe without blur (§5), `--r-md`, `--shadow-modal`, max 400 (confirm) / 640 (content); title display-lg; entering as §6 says. **Footers:** every modal carries one, at least a Neutral md "Close" (§8.38's grammar), so the scrim is never its only way out; md buttons, gap 8, right-aligned, primary last; in a bottom sheet lg, full width, stacked, primary last. Side sheets (evidence, decision log): 480 wide, the full viewport height at the right edge, sliding in its own width over `--t-med` and out over `--t-fast`, glass recipe without blur, `--r-lg` on the leading corners only. A side sheet carries no footer and instead a 56 header — title display-md, a Neutral 34 close IconButton (`xmark`) at the right, as in the full-height sheet's header below — at every width, for the same reason. **A side sheet is modal:** it carries the scrim, traps focus and closes on a scrim click — it is the overlay drawer (§4) that has no scrim, because a drawer over a document is for reading both. A modal is at most 90% of the viewport tall (§4 Height); both scroll inside themselves and keep their footer or header pinned, and a pinned footer or header carries no material of its own (§5). At 768–1023 a modal keeps its maxima, centred, and a side sheet keeps its 480. **Below 768 a modal and a side sheet are both bottom sheets:** glass recipe without blur, `--r-lg` on the top corners only, rising from the bottom edge over `--t-med`, a grabber — 36×4, `--n-disabled`, `--r-pill`, 8 from the top — above the content, `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` padded, the page behind never scrolling. Dismiss by the scrim, by Esc, or by the grabber: **the sheet follows the finger 1:1 downward and never above rest; release dismisses when the drag has passed 80 *or* the release velocity exceeds 0.5 px/ms downward** (a quick short flick is a dismissal to anyone who has used a sheet), the exit continuing from the finger's position over `--t-fast`; short of both it returns over `--t-med` on `--ease-spring`, its fill extending 8 below the viewport so the overshoot opens no seam. The velocity lives in `motion.ts` (§6). On pointer the grabber is not drawn and the sheet does not follow a drag — §7 gives pointer no swipe, and a grabber there would invite a drag it refuses (§4); the sheet closes by its footer or header, the scrim or Esc. **A full-height sheet** (the read-only transcript) drops the radius and keeps a 56 header — title display-md, a Neutral 34 close IconButton (`xmark`) at the right — so a drag is never the only way out. Focus is trapped inside a sheet exactly as inside a modal.

### 8.24 Check list

What the meter expands into: the whole rubric in pack order, passes included — product-spec §1 law 3's "a number that cannot be interrogated does not ship" rendered literally, so this is the canonical view of a run and not a list of complaints. One line per check: check id mono-readout `--n-secondary`, the check's own wording ui-body `--n-primary`, the state label pushed to the trailing edge; gap 8 between lines, 8 across a line and 4 when it wraps. Three line states:
- **Passed** — mono-micro `--success`, check icon 12, gap 4. Text label beside the tick, never the tick alone (§13). **The 12 is the sortable-header chevron's size, not a derivation from mono-micro's 14 line box:** a small in-text glyph beside a mono label is the same object here and in a table header, and re-deriving it from the type scale would "correct" it to 10 and break that pairing. Precedent, not a new value.
- **Unclear** — the §8.9 gap chip in its open tones, reading priority and state — "Must · unclear" in `--warning-soft` bg + `--warning` text, "Should · unclear" in `--surface-2` + `--n-secondary`. Only a Must is warm, because only a Must blocks; the word, not the warmth, is what says so (§13). The quoted evidence sits under the line on a `--surface-1` card in ui-body.
- **Not asked** — mono-micro `--n-secondary`, no container, with the condition beneath it in ui-footnote `--n-secondary`. product-spec §4 writes the condition affirmatively and the check is not asked precisely because it is false of the artifact, so the negation belongs to the frame around the condition, never to the condition itself.

**The meter block is bounded at 400** — the summary row carrying the meter, the mono-readout percentage and the freshness, and the unscored state at the same width so the track sits in one place whether or not there is a run behind it. §8.21 already names 400 as the confirm modal's max and the job is the same: a column that should not run to the full width of a page. **The expanded list below it takes the page column and is not separately bounded** — its measure is §3's reading rule, since a check's wording and a quoted evidence card are prose and a 400 column would wrap every line of it.

**A container means a gap, one level down as well.** Only the unclear line carries a chip; a pass and a not-asked check carry text labels without one. This is v2.15's item-row rule ("a bordered chip in a row must mean something, and what it means there is a gap") applied inside the expansion. **Not asked is deliberately not the excluded chip** even though both describe a check that left the denominator: exclusion is a negotiation outcome with a person's name on it (product-spec §5's first move), non-applicability is arithmetic the engine did. Giving them one treatment would make the machine's bookkeeping look like somebody's decision. **No Danger on this list at all** (§0 law 1, §12): an unclear Must is `--warning`, an unclear Should is neutral, and nothing here is red.

### 8.25 Disclosure

Native `<details>`/`<summary>` wherever a surface expands in place. Radius `--r-sm`, pad 8 vertical / 4 horizontal, gap 4 between chevron and summary text — this block is the single source for the summary's geometry; §8.10's accept form uses it unchanged. The browser marker is removed in both spellings (standards and WebKit): the affordance is the chevron, and a triangle beside it would be two. Chevron 16, **swapped rather than rotated** — `nav-arrow-right` closed, `nav-arrow-down` open — because §6 names no duration for a disclosure and v1 makes step changes instant. **Native, not a component:** the element already holds the only state a disclosure has, so this costs no client island, works with JavaScript off, and gets §11's keyboard path (Tab, Enter or Space) and screen-reader semantics correct without help. A disclosure that would open onto nothing is not rendered at all (§10).

### 8.27 Item row

56h: 2px bucket accent (`--prime` your-move / `--warning` at-risk / none flowing) → item name ui-headline + type → micro-meters → one gap chip + an overflow count → freshness dot + mono-readout timestamp → overflow menu. Idle: the name and readouts step to `--n-secondary` and the non-text parts — accent, meters, dots, chip fills — go to opacity .60 (§2 Dimming), and the freshness readout gives way to a Soft sm "Park?" in its place (one tap, undo toast) — that the item is idle is what the readout was saying, and the timestamp stands on the item page. The parked list renders the same way at .40. The name is the link to the item, and its hit area is the row's empty surface — a `::after` on the link covering the row, beneath the row's own controls — so modifier clicks on the row stay the browser's (§7) and the row remains a grid of controls (§11). The gap chip is a link to the item page with the check list expanded (§8.24) and that check scrolled into view.

**The row's addends, so the line count is arithmetic.** Inline padding 16, the 2px accent drawn inside it · column gap 8 (§8.24's in-row gap) · on lists that select or reorder, a reserved leading slot — the drag handle 16, gap 8, the checkbox 20 — so nothing moves when they appear on hover · the name at its 200 minimum, taking whatever remains · the type at most 80 (mono-micro; "ENHANCEMENT" is the longest, at 75) · the micro-meter group: one track per active stage, 16 wide, 4 between, budgeted at five stages — 96; a stage count above five moves the threshold and is a Decision · the chip column 160: one gap chip at most 112 wide ("Should · PRD-07" measures 110.7, which holds while a check id is at most six characters — the `PRD-07` form product-spec uses; a seventh character puts "Should · PRD-107" at 117.9 and is a Decision), gap 4, and an overflow count chip of 44 when there are more — the count is a number, so it speaks mono-readout (§0 law 6), and "+99" measures 41.6; three digits are a Decision, as a sixth stage is · the freshness column 72 — dot 8, gap 4, at most 8 characters of mono-readout ("14 d ago", §12); the idle row's "Park?" fits the same 72 · the overflow trigger 28 (a sm IconButton). Sum, with the leading slot and six gaps: 32 + 44 + 48 + 200 + 80 + 96 + 160 + 72 + 28 = **760**. One number for every list, taken from the fullest; a list without the slot gives its 52 to the name.

**Rows are a continuous ledger, not detached cards.** Within a bucket they sit flush on one `--surface-1` surface, divided by 1px `--bg-base` hairlines, with `--r-sm` applied to the *group* — top corners on the first row, bottom on the last, square between. The 2px accent runs unbroken down the group's left edge rather than restarting per row, which it can because a bucket is homogeneous by construction. Detached rounded rows read as separate objects that happen to be adjacent; the hairline is what makes eight entries one list. (This is the same construction §8.22 gives analytics tables, and for the same reason.)

**The type renders without a container** — plain mono-micro in `--n-secondary`, no outline. A bordered chip in a row must mean something, and what it means there is a gap. Type is taxonomy: it never changes and never needs action, so giving it the same outline as an open Must gap makes the one urgent element on the row compete with a permanent label.

**Below a content box of 760 the row is two lines, 72h,** decided by a container query on the list's own content box (§4 Grid), never by the viewport. **Height governs, as it does for buttons (§8.1):** each line is 28, since the tallest thing on either line is a sm control — the overflow trigger, the Park chip — and the two lines sit 4 apart, §8.24's wrap gap, so the one-line row centres 28 in 56 and the two-line row centres 60 in 72, 6 above and below. Where the break lands, from §4's columns: hand chrome narrower than 792 (the box is vw − 32); standard mode with the dock open narrower than 1428 (vw − 668); desk narrower than 1048 (vw − 288); wide always one line, since its box is 772 or more. The two-line row: the name (ui-headline, truncating) with the freshness readout at the trailing edge on the first line; the type (mono-micro), the micro-meters and one gap chip plus an overflow count on the second, with the overflow trigger at the second line's trailing edge. The leading slot stays at the leading edge, centred across both lines, and Right and Left keep the one-line row's order at both heights (§11), so a keyboard path never changes with the container's width. Below the read-only line (§4) the overflow trigger and the park chip are absent with every other mutation — the freshness readout takes the park chip's place back — and the tap opens the item. Rows never animate into a new order (§6).

**Paging and virtualization.** A bucket renders its first 50 rows and then a Secondary sm "Show more" centred beneath the group — never infinite scroll, which fights the bounded ledger and makes the footer unreachable. Past 200 rendered rows a list virtualizes, with the row height fixed by §4 so nothing jumps. Tables page at 25 with a mono-readout range at the foot ("1–25 of 312") and Neutral sm IconButtons for previous and next (`nav-arrow-left`, `nav-arrow-right`); the page is in the URL, so a page is a link.

**Bulk selection (triage, backlog).** Each row gains a leading Checkbox (20) in the reserved slot that appears on row hover and focus on pointer devices and is always rendered on touch. Selecting the first row raises the **selection bar**: a sticky bar (§4 rung 100) across the bottom of the list, 56h, glass recipe + `--scrim-bottom` placed per §4, pad 20, a mono-readout count at the left ("3 selected") beside a Ghost "Clear", and the bulk actions at the right as Secondary sm — a destructive one as Danger sm behind its confirm step. `Shift`-click on a checkbox selects a range from the last checked row; `Cmd/Ctrl+A` selects every rendered row of the focused list — not the rows behind Show more; Esc clears; on touch the checkboxes are the whole interface. The bar leaves when the count reaches zero. A selected row keeps its hairline ledger geometry and takes `--prime-soft` as its fill.

### 8.34 Agent states

Working, streaming, settling, pending. Before the first token the agent's turn is a **working line**: a 16 Spinner in `--agent` beside a mono-readout `--n-secondary` phrase naming the step in lowercase — "reading the PRD", "running checks", "drafting" — appearing after `--delay-working` so a fast reply never flashes it, standing until the first token replaces it. It never times out into an apology; if the provider fails, the line becomes the §10 error line, "Couldn't reach the model — retry", with its Secondary sm Retry.
**Streaming.** Text appends in place as it arrives, in the message's own grammar — ui-body on Base for the agent's speech, the violet proposal card for anything that will become content — behind a steady **caret**: a 2×18 `--agent` bar after the last glyph, removed with the final token. It does not blink; §0 law 1 is absolute, and the caret's presence rather than its rhythm says "still writing". Formatting renders as it arrives, and an unclosed code fence renders as mono-code from its opening line rather than waiting to close. The transcript **follows the stream only while the reader is at the bottom** — within 24 of the transcript's end; once they have scrolled up it holds, and a Soft sm "Jump to latest" chip with an `arrow-down` floats bottom-centre of the dock, 16 above the composer, until it is tapped or the bottom is reached; the follow itself is instant (§6). A stream can be stopped: the composer's send IconButton becomes the `square` stop for the stream's duration (§8.33), and a stopped reply keeps what arrived with a mono-readout "stopped" trailing it.
**Settling.** A proposal card the person confirms drops its violet over `--t-med` (§8.32); one they set aside collapses over `--t-fast` (§6 — `grid-template-rows` to `0fr`) and leaves a ui-footnote `--n-secondary` line "Set aside" in its place for the rest of the session, so the transcript never has a hole where a decision was.
**Optimistic or pending.** Human moves split by reversibility. *Optimistic* — park, confirm, accept, reopen, decide-later: the interface applies the change at once and offers the undo toast; on failure it reverts, dismisses that move's undo at once and the toast says what did not happen ("Couldn't park — try again"), leading dot `--warning`, never red. Every optimistic move has a standing reversal on the page besides the toast (§8.20). *Pending* — anything irreversible or external: signing a packet, sending a code, any write that leaves aenima takes the button's loading state (spinner replaces label, width locked) and applies only on the server's answer; nothing shows as done before it is. Agent-initiated external writes always pass through a confirm first (§0 law 4), so the agent has no optimistic state of its own.

## 9. Charts

Series order for data with no meaning attached: `--prime` · `--n-secondary` · `--prime-deep` · `--n-primary` — four neutral series at most; a fifth is grouped as "other" or the chart is split. `--agent`, `--success` and `--warning` enter a chart only where the series *is* agent output, quality or risk — §0 law 3 keeps violet off human data, and a two-series chart with violet in second place would paint it there. ## 10. System states & degraded pages

- **No AI key:** meters render hollow tracks + "Connect AI to activate scoring" (ui-footnote) — never zeros, never red. **That is the item page, where the line stands beside the track and says what the emptiness means.** A list row has no room for the line, so the hollow track there is an unlabelled stub repeated once per row — noise that explains nothing. **Row micro-meters do not render at all without a key**; the space goes to the content, and the meters appear when the scores do.
- **Provider outage / retry:** freshness shows `--warning` dot + mono-readout "scored 6 h ago — retrying" on the item page; a row shows the `--warning` dot alone, its readout unchanged; no banners.
- **404/500/offline:** empty-state pattern on dot grid, Æ mark 32, one line, one action ("Back to dashboard" / "Retry"). 500 may show a mono-readout incident id.
- Full-page loads: skeleton screens mirroring the target layout — the content column only, never the chrome, which renders from the route before any data (§4) — never a centered spinner page. **Content replaces its skeleton in place and fades in over `--t-fast`; the skeleton leaves in the same frame.** Route changes are instant (§6, §8.3).
- **Every data-bound component carries three states of its own, at its own size.** *Loading* is a skeleton of the component's own shape — rows for a list, a track for a meter, a bar for a chart — never a spinner where a list will be. *Empty* is the compact empty state: one ui-body `--n-secondary` line at the component's own padding and at most one action, no icon, no texture — the full §8.37 empty state with its icon and dot grid is for a page, not a card. *Error* is a ui-footnote `--n-secondary` line naming what did not load and a Secondary sm "Retry" — never `--danger`, because a load failure is not the person's error and §0 law 2 reserves red for what is. A page never fails as a whole because one component did.

## 11. Keyboard & focus

`Cmd/Ctrl+K` opens the chat if it is closed and focuses the composer; pressed while the composer already has focus it closes the drawer, or in the docked modes returns focus to where it came from (§8.33) · `Esc` closes the most recently opened layer (last-opened-wins, per §4 — not the highest rung) and never changes the route · `Enter` on a proposal's confirm button confirms it — the card's buttons are its Tab stops, and a card is never focusable itself — and `Cmd/Ctrl+Enter` sends in the composer · `Cmd/Ctrl+Z` triggers a showing undo toast when focus is not in a text field (§8.20) · arrow keys walk menus/selects/list rows · `/` focuses search in list views · `?` opens the shortcut sheet (§8.38) · a focused row's overflow menu is reached with Right (the grid model below) — the `ContextMenu` key and `Shift+F10` stay the browser's, as right-click does (§7, §16). **Global shortcuts work regardless of what has focus** — the reason a console gives the guide its own button — with two exceptions: the unmodified keys (`/`, `?`) and `Cmd/Ctrl+Z` are inert while focus is in a text field, where they are typing; and while a modal or sheet is open only Esc and the layer's own keys act, since a shortcut that moved focus out of the trap would land in the inert page. `Cmd/Ctrl+K` acts everywhere else; `Cmd/Ctrl+Enter` belongs to the composer alone. **Focus starts in the content.** On a fresh load the first Tab stop is a "Skip to content" link — a Neutral sm pill (not Soft: in hand chrome it stands over the top bar's glass, the toast's case, §8.20), visible only while focused, at the top-left of the viewport inset 16 on both axes, rung 600 — ahead of the sidebar's seven stops (lockup, switcher, four nav items, account), or in hand chrome the top bar's four (mark, switcher, chat, menu). After a route change the code focuses the main region (`tabindex="-1"`), so the next Tab lands on the page's first control and a screen reader announces the page by its title; **the main region never draws the ring, whatever the modality** — it is a landmark, not a control, and the ring is a control's affordance — the one exemption from §6's rule and C-31. A console chooses where focus starts so that reaching anything costs the fewest presses; the web's default of starting at the top of the document is the opposite choice. **The way back restores the place.** Returning to a list — by Back, by the breadcrumb, by the sidebar — restores its scroll position and, instead of the main region, focuses the row that was opened (with the ring under keyboard modality, since a row is a control); a filter or page in the URL (§8.27, §8.29) restores with it; the dock keeps its scroll and its remembered control (§4). Panels and sheets never remember: they reopen fresh. **The Esc stack** holds, in opening order: panels, modals and sheets, the overlay drawer, a live selection. A showing tooltip takes Esc first and closes nothing else; an active drag takes it before the stack — Esc cancels the drag and closes nothing. Toasts are not in it; a toast never takes focus on arrival — `Cmd/Ctrl+Z` is the keyboard path to its undo, and Tab reaches it last in document order. **Menus, selects and tabs carry roving focus:** one Tab stop; arrows move within and wrap; Home and End jump; a typed letter jumps to the next match; Enter or Space activates; Esc closes a panel and returns focus to its trigger; Tab closes the panel and carries focus to the stop after the trigger, Shift+Tab to the one before — in a select, committing the active option first. **A list is one Tab stop and a grid inside:** Up and Down move between rows; Right and Left walk the focused row's controls in the one-line row's visual order, at both row heights — handle, checkbox, name, chip, on an idle row the Park chip, overflow trigger; Enter opens the item from the name; Home and End jump; in a selectable list Space toggles the focused row, `Shift`+Up/Down extend the selection, `Cmd/Ctrl+A` takes every rendered row. On a drag handle Space grabs, arrows move, Space drops, Esc cancels. Every interactive element reachable by keyboard in visual order; focus ring per §6; focus trapped inside modals and sheets; on close, focus returns to the opener — after a morph, the trigger the panel grew from.

## 13. Accessibility

- On Base, `--n-primary` 15.7:1 and `--n-secondary` 7.9:1; `--prime` text 8.5:1; `--agent` 7.4:1; `--warning` 9.7:1; `--danger` 7.5:1. Re-measured on the v2.8 ramp, where the deeper base lifts every one of them. Read against the worst surface in the ramp rather than Base, the same six are 11.1 · 5.5 · 6.0 · 5.2 · 6.8 · 5.3 — all clear AA, and the accents clear 5.1. **Placeholder and Disabled fail AA by design** — never information-carrying.
- Accent text/icons always use bright tones; deep tones are fills with labels on them.
- Meters always pair color with the numeric value; gap states carry text labels, not color alone.
- Focus-visible on everything; full keyboard paths (list → item → chat → sign); `prefers-reduced-motion` per §6. **Text on glass is measured on `--glass-fill` over `--prime` with no scrim** — the brightest large fill that can pass under a panel or a toast, which carry no scrim — and C-29 computes it: at the fill's .85, `--n-secondary` holds 4.77:1 there, and a sticky bar's scrim (§4) only adds margin. The fill is .85 rather than .80 because at .80 that stack measures 4.32, under AA.
- Dimming never takes text below AA (§2): idle and parked rows and settled gap cards step their text to `--n-secondary` and dim only their non-text parts.
- Under `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` every glass surface takes §5's fallback; under `prefers-contrast: more` it does the same and `--glass-border` becomes `--n-secondary`. The first query matches in one engine today, so both are enhancements and neither gates.
- **Text zoom.** At 200% browser zoom every string stays readable and whole and no control clips: the px breakpoints (§4) carry the layout into the narrower mode, rows take their two lines, and nothing is exempt — the PS5's "larger text" enlarges some of its interface and not the rest, and the reviewers who needed it noticed at once. Zoom is a width change, so the read-only line (§4) is where it bites: a 1280 laptop at 200% is 640 wide, which is why the line is 600 on pointer rather than 768 — a zoomed desktop keeps its writes; a phone does not.
- Floating labels are real `<label>` elements bound to their inputs at every moment of the animation — the at-rest state is a styled label, never a placeholder standing in for one.
- Menus are `role="menu"` with `menuitem` rows; selects and comboboxes are `combobox` with a `listbox`; lists are a `grid` of one Tab stop (§11); the streaming transcript is `aria-live="polite"` with `aria-busy` true for the stream's duration, so a screen reader hears a reply once, whole, rather than every token. Drag is fully keyboard-operable and announces each move. Hit areas are 44 on touch and 40 on pointer — menu and option rows are their own hit areas, exempt from the pointer floor, and everything smaller extends its hit area (§7 Hit areas). A view transition never moves focus on its own: the code places it inside the update callback, on the panel's first item — a select's selected option, a combobox's input (§6).
