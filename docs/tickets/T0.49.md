  compare — the stylesheet and one module under `src/lib` — and nowhere else (T0.48's §4).

  drops a urlencoded action POST and, with JavaScript off, the move silently does not happen
  (T2.5).

  formatter functions, and one handed to `ItemRowMenu`'s kind of component is a production 500
  that no unit test and no client-rooted preview catches (`docs/log/production-500-fix.md`).

  gains no client island — `WriteGate` is the one it has.

## 2. Color system

Dark-only. Every accent that carries a label has two tones: **fill** (surface color; label sits on it) and **bright** (text/icon/stroke on dark, ≥7:1 on Base). Softs are the bright tone at low alpha. Copy-paste tokens:

```css
:root {
  color-scheme: dark; /* the native date picker, default scrollbars and form
                         internals render dark; without this line they render light */
  /* surfaces */
  /* The ramp is deepened for OLED-safe depth — never #000, which smears on
     OLED and halates against light type. The base→surface-1 gap is widened
     deliberately so fields lift off the page instead of sitting in it. Every
     AA-required pair is re-verified ≥4.5 at these values, accents read ≥5.1 as
     text on every surface, and dark labels on accent fills clear 7 under the
```

### 8.10 Gap moves

product-spec §5's negotiation moves on a gap, wherever that gap is drawn — the gap card (§8.32) and the check list render the same control, never two.
- **The settled stamp** is ui-footnote `--n-secondary`: who accepted or excluded it, and their reason after an em dash. product-spec §1 law 7 makes a debt something a *named* person owns, so the name travels with the gap rather than living only on the card.
- **Accept** is a disclosure (§8.25) with a ui-footnote `--n-secondary` summary. Inside it, §8.2's field grammar unchanged — 48h pill, floating label, one reserved 18h helper line — and a **Primary md** submit.
- **Reopen** is a standing control beside the stamp, **Secondary sm and not Ghost**: a settled gap card is already dimmed (§8.32), and §8.3's own warning about a text-only control that "vanishes precisely when it has the most to say" is exactly this case. §0 law 7 dims it; nothing disables it.
- **Neither move takes a confirm step.** §0 law 2 reserves that for destructive actions, and accepting is not destructive, reopening is not either, and both have standing reversals.
- **Danger appears on exactly one thing here:** the reason field's own helper line on a validation error, which §0 law 2 names as one of its three sanctioned uses. Every other outcome a move can report is about the request rather than the field, and renders ui-footnote `--n-secondary` beside the form.

**A move's confirmation renders outside the disclosure its own outcome closes.** The accept form's `open` is decided by the outcome — a move that landed needs no form open behind it — so a message placed inside would be hidden by precisely the outcomes it exists to report. A disclosure decides whether the *form* is open and never whether a sentence is readable. This is a general rule about disclosures, not a note about one form: it shipped broken once, with a reversal's confirmation in the DOM inside a collapsed element, reaching nobody, on the one move product-spec §1 law 4 exists to make visible.

### 8.24 Check list

What the meter expands into: the whole rubric in pack order, passes included — product-spec §1 law 3's "a number that cannot be interrogated does not ship" rendered literally, so this is the canonical view of a run and not a list of complaints. One line per check: check id mono-readout `--n-secondary`, the check's own wording ui-body `--n-primary`, the state label pushed to the trailing edge; gap 8 between lines, 8 across a line and 4 when it wraps. Three line states:
- **Passed** — mono-micro `--success`, check icon 12, gap 4. Text label beside the tick, never the tick alone (§13). **The 12 is the sortable-header chevron's size, not a derivation from mono-micro's 14 line box:** a small in-text glyph beside a mono label is the same object here and in a table header, and re-deriving it from the type scale would "correct" it to 10 and break that pairing. Precedent, not a new value.
- **Unclear** — the §8.9 gap chip in its open tones, reading priority and state — "Must · unclear" in `--warning-soft` bg + `--warning` text, "Should · unclear" in `--surface-2` + `--n-secondary`. Only a Must is warm, because only a Must blocks; the word, not the warmth, is what says so (§13). The quoted evidence sits under the line on a `--surface-1` card in ui-body.
- **Not asked** — mono-micro `--n-secondary`, no container, with the condition beneath it in ui-footnote `--n-secondary`. product-spec §4 writes the condition affirmatively and the check is not asked precisely because it is false of the artifact, so the negation belongs to the frame around the condition, never to the condition itself.

**The meter block is bounded at 400** — the summary row carrying the meter, the mono-readout percentage and the freshness, and the unscored state at the same width so the track sits in one place whether or not there is a run behind it. §8.21 already names 400 as the confirm modal's max and the job is the same: a column that should not run to the full width of a page. **The expanded list below it takes the page column and is not separately bounded** — its measure is §3's reading rule, since a check's wording and a quoted evidence card are prose and a 400 column would wrap every line of it.

**A container means a gap, one level down as well.** Only the unclear line carries a chip; a pass and a not-asked check carry text labels without one. This is v2.15's item-row rule ("a bordered chip in a row must mean something, and what it means there is a gap") applied inside the expansion. **Not asked is deliberately not the excluded chip** even though both describe a check that left the denominator: exclusion is a negotiation outcome with a person's name on it (product-spec §5's first move), non-applicability is arithmetic the engine did. Giving them one treatment would make the machine's bookkeeping look like somebody's decision. **No Danger on this list at all** (§0 law 1, §12): an unclear Must is `--warning`, an unclear Should is neutral, and nothing here is red.

### 8.25 Disclosure

Native `<details>`/`<summary>` wherever a surface expands in place. The summary is a control in §7's sense — it carries the shared control class, so §7's states arrive whole: hover overlay, press physics, and the focus ring *with* the `--prime-glow` §6 and §7 pair it with — with the specular edge off, since that edge is Primary's alone. Radius `--r-sm`, pad 8 vertical / 4 horizontal, gap 4 between chevron and summary text — this block is the single source for the summary's geometry; §8.10's accept form uses it unchanged. The browser marker is removed in both spellings (standards and WebKit): the affordance is the chevron, and a triangle beside it would be two. Chevron 16, **swapped rather than rotated** — `nav-arrow-right` closed, `nav-arrow-down` open — because §6 names no duration for a disclosure and v1 makes step changes instant. **Native, not a component:** the element already holds the only state a disclosure has, so this costs no client island, works with JavaScript off, and gets §11's keyboard path (Tab, Enter or Space) and screen-reader semantics correct without help. A disclosure that would open onto nothing is not rendered at all (§10).

### 8.27 Item row

56h: 2px bucket accent (`--prime` your-move / `--warning` at-risk / none flowing) → item name ui-headline + type → micro-meters → one gap chip + an overflow count → freshness dot + mono-readout timestamp → overflow menu. Idle: the name and readouts step to `--n-secondary` and the non-text parts — accent, meters, dots, chip fills — go to opacity .60 (§2 Dimming), and the freshness readout gives way to a Soft sm "Park?" in its place (one tap, undo toast) — that the item is idle is what the readout was saying, and the timestamp stands on the item page. The parked list renders the same way at .40. The name is the link to the item, and its hit area is the row's empty surface — a `::after` on the link covering the row, beneath the row's own controls — so modifier clicks on the row stay the browser's (§7) and the row remains a grid of controls (§11). The gap chip is a link to the item page with the check list expanded (§8.24) and that check scrolled into view.

**The row's addends, so the line count is arithmetic.** Inline padding 16, the 2px accent drawn inside it · column gap 8 (§8.24's in-row gap) · on lists that select or reorder, a reserved leading slot — the drag handle 16, gap 8, the checkbox 20 — so nothing moves when they appear on hover · the name at its 200 minimum, taking whatever remains · the type at most 80 (mono-micro; "ENHANCEMENT" is the longest, at 75) · the micro-meter group: one track per active stage, 16 wide, 4 between, budgeted at five stages — 96; a stage count above five moves the threshold and is a Decision · the chip column 160: one gap chip at most 112 wide ("Should · PRD-07" measures 110.7, which holds while a check id is at most six characters — the `PRD-07` form product-spec uses; a seventh character puts "Should · PRD-107" at 117.9 and is a Decision), gap 4, and an overflow count chip of 44 when there are more — the count is a number, so it speaks mono-readout (§0 law 6), and "+99" measures 41.6; three digits are a Decision, as a sixth stage is · the freshness column 72 — dot 8, gap 4, at most 8 characters of mono-readout ("14 d ago", §12); the idle row's "Park?" fits the same 72 · the overflow trigger 28 (a sm IconButton). Sum, with the leading slot and six gaps: 32 + 44 + 48 + 200 + 80 + 96 + 160 + 72 + 28 = **760**. One number for every list, taken from the fullest; a list without the slot gives its 52 to the name.

**Rows are a continuous ledger, not detached cards.** Within a bucket they sit flush on one `--surface-1` surface, divided by 1px `--bg-base` hairlines, with `--r-sm` applied to the *group* — top corners on the first row, bottom on the last, square between. The 2px accent runs unbroken down the group's left edge rather than restarting per row, which it can because a bucket is homogeneous by construction. Detached rounded rows read as separate objects that happen to be adjacent; the hairline is what makes eight entries one list. (This is the same construction §8.22 gives analytics tables, and for the same reason.)

**The type renders without a container** — plain mono-micro in `--n-secondary`, no outline. A bordered chip in a row must mean something, and what it means there is a gap. Type is taxonomy: it never changes and never needs action, so giving it the same outline as an open Must gap makes the one urgent element on the row compete with a permanent label.

**Below a content box of 760 the row is two lines, 72h,** decided by a container query on the list's own content box (§4 Grid), never by the viewport. **Height governs, as it does for buttons (§8.1):** each line is 28, since the tallest thing on either line is a sm control — the overflow trigger, the Park chip — and the two lines sit 4 apart, §8.24's wrap gap, so the one-line row centres 28 in 56 and the two-line row centres 60 in 72, 6 above and below. Where the break lands, from §4's columns: hand chrome narrower than 792 (the box is vw − 32); standard mode with the dock open narrower than 1428 (vw − 668); desk narrower than 1048 (vw − 288); wide always one line, since its box is 772 or more. The two-line row: the name (ui-headline, truncating) with the freshness readout at the trailing edge on the first line; the type (mono-micro), the micro-meters and one gap chip plus an overflow count on the second, with the overflow trigger at the second line's trailing edge. The leading slot stays at the leading edge, centred across both lines, and Right and Left keep the one-line row's order at both heights (§11), so a keyboard path never changes with the container's width. Below the read-only line (§4) the overflow trigger and the park chip are absent with every other mutation — the freshness readout takes the park chip's place back — and the tap opens the item. Rows never animate into a new order (§6).

**Paging and virtualization.** A bucket renders its first 50 rows and then a Secondary sm "Show more" centred beneath the group — never infinite scroll, which fights the bounded ledger and makes the footer unreachable. Past 200 rendered rows a list virtualizes, with the row height fixed by §4 so nothing jumps. Tables page at 25 with a mono-readout range at the foot ("1–25 of 312") and Neutral sm IconButtons for previous and next (`nav-arrow-left`, `nav-arrow-right`); the page is in the URL, so a page is a link.

**Bulk selection (triage, backlog).** Each row gains a leading Checkbox (20) in the reserved slot that appears on row hover and focus on pointer devices and is always rendered on touch. Selecting the first row raises the **selection bar**: a sticky bar (§4 rung 100) across the bottom of the list, 56h, glass recipe + `--scrim-bottom` placed per §4, pad 20, a mono-readout count at the left ("3 selected") beside a Ghost "Clear", and the bulk actions at the right as Secondary sm — a destructive one as Danger sm behind its confirm step. `Shift`-click on a checkbox selects a range from the last checked row; `Cmd/Ctrl+A` selects every rendered row of the focused list — not the rows behind Show more; Esc clears; on touch the checkboxes are the whole interface. The bar leaves when the count reaches zero. A selected row keeps its hairline ledger geometry and takes `--prime-soft` as its fill.

### 8.32 Cards

Per §5. **The card's buttons, wherever a card carries moves:** confirm is Primary sm ("Looks right"); set aside, decide later and redirect are Ghost sm; undo lives in the toast, never on the card. **Agent-proposal card:** `--agent-soft` tint + 2px `--agent` left border + confirm; on confirm, violet drops and the card settles to a plain `--surface-1` card (`--t-med` transition — the moment of "human accepted" is visible). **Triage card:** an agent-proposal card — `--agent-soft` tint, 2px `--agent` left border — since its destination is the agent's proposal (§0 law 4): the source icon (a brand glyph, §8.11), the extract quote in ui-body, the proposed destination and confirm/redirect; the violet drops on confirm or redirect, as on any proposal. **Gap card** — a gap drawn on its own, in the transcript or on the item page: a plain card, padding per §5 by where it stands, the check's wording ui-body, its gap chip (§8.9), the quoted evidence on a `--surface-1` card in ui-body, and §8.10's moves at the foot; once accepted or excluded it carries the settled stamp and dims per §2 — text to `--n-secondary`, the non-text parts at .60 — and its Reopen stays fully live. **Time estimate:** a gap card and a walkthrough card carry one ui-footnote `--n-secondary` line beneath the wording, "Typically {n} min" (§12), the number in mono-readout, where product-spec has a typical time for the move; it is small information, never a headline — product-spec's own ruling — and it never renders without a number. The PS5's Activity cards carry a playtime estimate for the same reason: knowing a move takes ten minutes is what makes a person start it. **A card never truncates the text that says what it is for:** its title wraps to two lines before §3's truncation reaches it, and its wording is prose and never truncates; the PS5's cards, which cut trophy names mid-word, are the counter-example.
