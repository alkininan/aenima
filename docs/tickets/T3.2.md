# T3.2 — Chat panel

Spec: product-spec v1.8 §6, §1, §13 · design-spec v2.21 §4 Chat placement, §8.32, §8.33,
§8.34, §6, §11 · §17 C-21, C-22, C-24, C-30, C-33, C-36, C-43, C-47

## Objective

The item page's chat dock, where the interview actually happens: the author asks, the human
answers in plain speech, every answer comes back as the sentence that will enter the document,
and nothing is written until the human confirms it.

## Build

**The echo is editable.** An answer is echoed back in doc-language, and the human either
confirms it, edits it and confirms, or rejects it. Editable rather than confirm-or-reject is this
ticket's decision: a wrong word costs one edit instead of a whole re-answer, and the edited text
is the human's, not the agent's — so it lands without violet attribution while a confirmed echo
keeps it until the section settles.

**Attribution until confirmed.** Agent-authored text carries design-spec's violet for
agent-authored-until-confirmed, everywhere it appears — the echo, the drafted section, the
proposal. Confirmation clears it. Text the human typed or edited never wears it.

**Decide later is a first-class answer.** §6: it is always legal. The question lands in an
auto-rendered Open Questions section naming the check it blocks, the meter reflects the document
as it stands, and the interview moves to the next question rather than pressing.

**Proposals, not actions.** Anything with a side effect — park, log a decision, exclude a check,
push a gap — is proposed in the dock and happens on confirm, undoably, per §1's fourth law. The
dock is a full action surface (§13), not a text box.

**One question at a time**, in the order §6 sets: the problem block first, then whichever
question closes the most remaining rubric weight. A single enthusiastic paragraph is credited
against every check it touches rather than asked about again.

**The dock is a client island** — the item page's first. Keep it bounded: the page stays a Server
Component and the dock is the only interactive region, so T1.3's read-only page is not unwound.

## Rules

- Nothing with a side effect happens without a confirm, and everything is undoable
- Violet means agent-authored-until-confirmed and nothing else; never invent a colour or a
  duration — design-spec has them
- Welcoming, never alarming: "good catch", "this section was unclear"
- The question order is computed in code — remaining weight is arithmetic, not judgement
- Decide later is never discouraged, never re-asked in the same session

## Criteria

- AC1 A confirmed echo enters the document as written, and the violet attribution clears
- AC2 An edited echo enters as the human's text and never carries attribution
- AC3 A rejected echo writes nothing and the question is re-asked once, differently
- AC4 Decide later writes an open question naming the blocked check and moves on
- AC5 A proposal with a side effect does nothing until confirmed, and the confirm is undoable
- AC6 One answer covering three checks is credited against all three and none is re-asked
- AC7 The item page ships exactly one client island

## Addendum — three corrections to T3.1's defaults (2026-09-20)

Carried here because this is the next claim on the same code. All three are rulings, not
questions.

**1. A surfaced check closes for the artifact version, not forever.** T3.1 closes a surfaced
check on that section permanently. Right within a session, wrong across versions: if the human
later rewrites the section, the check must get another look rather than staying shut on a
judgement about text that no longer exists. Key the closure to (artifact version, section,
check); a new version of that section reopens it at round zero. The surfaced round row is
untouched — it is history, and history does not change.

**2. Checks in play come from the version being refined.** T3.1 reads them from the artifact's
newest scoring run, falling back to the unconditioned set when never scored. A section refined
before its first re-score is then judged against a stale set of applicable checks, which
contradicts §5: results cache per artifact version, so applicability belongs to the version too.
Read the checks in play from the scoring run for the version under refinement; where that version
has no run, the set is the unconditioned one, as now.

**3. An over-long objection is never discarded silently.** T3.1 drops an objection that will not
fit a round. A critic objection is the product's whole value — a dropped one is a gap nobody
hears about, which is "nothing passes downstream unknowingly" broken by a size limit. Truncate
the reason and keep the binding, the evidence quote and the scope, which is what the round
actually needs; record that it was truncated on the round row; and if the evidence quote itself
cannot fit, surface the objection to the human rather than dropping it. Nothing about this path
may be silent.

**AA1** a new version of a section reopens a surfaced check at round zero, and the old surfaced
row is unchanged · **AA2** the checks in play match the scoring run for the version under
refinement, not the newest · **AA3** an over-long objection is truncated-with-record or surfaced,
never dropped; a test asserts no path discards one · TA1 → AA1 · TA2 → AA2 · TA3 → AA3

## Cited

### product-spec §1

## 1. Principles

Every mechanic below follows these laws. Where a conflict appears, the law wins.

1. **Status is derived, never declared.** The system infers an item's stage from which artifacts exist and what they score. No human ever sets or updates a status field.
2. **Advisory everywhere, hard at one gate.** Readiness scores inform; they never block movement — except the development handover, which requires explicit sign-off.
3. **Evidence or nothing.** Every score, flag, and suggestion expands into the exact quoted gap. A number that cannot be interrogated does not ship.
4. **Agent proposes, human confirms.** The AI never silently adds, excludes, closes, or files anything with side effects. One-tap confirms, always undoable. Until confirmed, agent-authored content is visibly attributed to the agent (the design system's violet).
5. **Scores move only when reality moves.** Check results are cached per artifact version; only checks whose underlying artifact changed are re-evaluated. The scoring model is pinned per workspace.
6. **Welcoming, never alarming.** Language is "walkthrough / good catch / this section was unclear," never "test / fail / violation." Idle work dims; it never turns red.
7. **Nothing is ever unknowingly passed downstream.** Gaps, exclusions, and flags are visible debts that a named person accepts. Freedom is total; deniability is zero.
8. **English pivot.** All ingested content gets an English working copy for reasoning; originals are preserved as evidence. Outputs render in the workspace language (EN/TR/NL). Prompt packs default to English.
9. **One editing surface per document at a time.** Aenima holds immutable versions; the mirrored tool (Notion) is the editing surface. No bidirectional merge logic exists.
10. **The system meets teams where they are.** Every stage is skippable, every entry state (blank, upload, connected, live product) works, and skipped checks simply resurface later.
11. **Full export from day one.** Items, scores, versions, decisions, and sign-off records export as JSON + documents.

### product-spec §6

## 6. Authoring engine

Documents are created by interview, not by template-filling. Two agents run every session:

- The **author** conducts: drafts from whatever exists, asks the next unanswered question in plain speech, one at a time.
- The **critic** runs behind every answer, blind: it sees only the artifact and the rubric — never the conversation, the author's confidence, or the owner's pushback. It detects and cites; it never rewrites. Every objection must bind to a rubric check ID; unbound objections are discarded (the structural cure for nitpicking). An objection carries four things — the check id it binds to, the reason, the quoted evidence, and its **scope**: which section it may change. A returned section is corrected, not improved. Without a scope the author opens the section, notices two adjacent weaknesses, fixes those too, and a one-check correction becomes a rewrite nobody asked for — which also invalidates the check results cached against the sections it touched. The two-round cap governs attempts; scope governs blast radius, and both are needed.

Session flow, identical at every entry state:

1. **Collect.** "Got anything on this already — a doc, a thread, or just what's in your head?" Upload, pick from connected sources, or keep talking. Skipped when intake already routed material.
2. **Silent test.** The critic runs all checks against everything collected, including the braindump itself. The meter jumps to its true starting score: "14 of 20 already answered from your doc."
3. **Gap interview.** Only the leftovers are asked. The problem block always opens first (everything downstream inherits a wrong problem); after that, whichever question closes the most remaining rubric weight goes next. Each answer is echoed back in doc-language for a one-tap confirm before it's written. A single enthusiastic paragraph is credited against every check it touches.
4. **Close — whenever.** Sessions persist; the meter holds. "Decide later" is always legal and lands in an auto-rendered Open Questions section with the check it blocks.

Hard limits from the self-correction research: **two refinement rounds per section, maximum** — after two, the disagreement surfaces to the human as an open question. Author and critic are separated so the judge is never anchored by the drafting context.

The screen: item page, conversation on one side, the draft document assembling live on the other, meter on top. More material captured upstream (meetings, Slack, braindumps) → fewer questions asked → the system visibly rewards being fed.

### product-spec §13

## 13. Surface

**Primary view: a prioritized list, not a board.** Status is derived, so a drag-to-move board would be decoration. Three buckets:

- **Your move** — anything awaiting a human: sign-offs, exclusion confirms, triage items, walkthrough answers, stalled packets (>48 h). Always on top.
- **At risk** — score regressed this week, a handover-blocking gap older than 5 days, or time-in-stage past ~1.5× the learned baseline. Sorted by blocking-gap age 40%, regression size 30%, staleness ratio 20%, handover proximity 10% (weights tunable).
- **Flowing** — everything else, by recent activity.

A thin pipeline strip on top shows counts per stage. Each row carries per-stage readiness meters. Idle items dim relative to their stage baseline and receive a one-tap, reversible **park** suggestion; the parked list ("graveyard") is a first-class, guilt-free destination — killing ideas cheaply is the system working. Retroactive imports keep historical gaps in a separate **foundation gaps** view with one-tap "adopt," never polluting the buckets.

**Chat is everywhere and is a full action surface.** It is also the command palette: `Cmd/Ctrl+K` focuses it from any page. Globally aware (whole workspace), page-aware only for pronoun resolution ("park this" needs no name on an item page; "how's Juno doing?" works anywhere and navigates you there while answering). It performs every permitted move — park, confirm exclusions, log decisions, push gaps, draft patches — and respects the role matrix: a Developer asking for a rubric change gets "that's an Owner action — want me to request it?" It answers ceremony questions in librarian mode and captures decision moments ("so we're dropping video for V1 because of capacity") with an offer to log them: decision, reason, date, who.

**Notifications route by role.** Owner: daily digest + spend alerts. Product: triage, exclusion confirms, regressions. Developer: packets and walkthrough follow-ups only. Viewer: nothing by default. The **daily digest reports deltas, never state dumps:** new blocking findings, resolved findings, regressions, aging sign-offs, ready-buffer line, glossary proposals — in the same neutral voice throughout.

- **Subtitle slot.** Any page or step title may carry one subtitle line beneath it: ui-body, `--n-secondary`, 8 below the title. Instructional copy ("We'll send a six-digit code") lives here, and only here — never in a field's helper line (§8.2). One line; it truncates rather than wraps on narrow widths — except where the subtitle carries an address the person must check: the OTP subtitle wraps to as many lines as the address needs and never truncates it, since touch has no tooltip and no page holds that string as content.
- **Z-ladder** (never improvise): content 0 · sticky bars 100 · chat dock 200 · dropdown/popover 300 · modal scrim+modal 400 · toast 500 · tooltip and the skip link (§11) 600. **The ladder governs painting only.** Dismissal is last-opened-wins: `Esc` closes the most recently opened layer regardless of its rung, so a select opened inside a modal takes the first `Esc` and the modal takes the second. Ranking dismissal by rung would strand the inner layer. **The ladder governs what is not in the browser's top layer.** Popovers and dialogs paint in the top layer in the order they opened, above every rung — which puts a menu above the modal it was opened from, the same answer the ladder gives. Toasts and tooltips are `popover="manual"` so they too live there. Top-layer order is opening order and a modal makes everything outside its own subtree inert, so a toast is not left to that order: **the toast host is a `popover="manual"` that is moved into the topmost open modal's subtree — as its last child, and otherwise the last child of `body` — and re-shown whenever a modal opens or closes,** so it paints above the scrim, stays operable and is last in document order for Tab; its clock carries over the move. A tooltip is created in the same place — inside the topmost open modal's subtree, otherwise in `body` — so one raised over a modal's control can be hovered (§8.14). The rung numbers remain the tie-break for everything outside the top layer — sticky bars, the selection bar, the dock.
- **Overlay interior.** Modals, sheets, and toasts pad 20; stacked content inside them gaps on the 8-grid. Panels (menu, select, combobox, popover) originate on their trigger and carry no gap — the trigger sits inside the panel's footprint for as long as it is open (§6 Morph).
- **Scrollbars.** 8px thin; thumb `--surface-2`, hover `--glass-border`; track transparent. Mechanism: `scrollbar-color` and `scrollbar-width: thin` inside `@supports not selector(::-webkit-scrollbar)`, and the 8px thumb with its `--glass-border` hover through `::-webkit-scrollbar` elsewhere — an engine that honours the standard properties discards every `::-webkit-scrollbar` rule on the same element, so the two must never meet. Firefox gets no hover colour. The gate is Firefox's answer to `selector(::-webkit-scrollbar)`, which has changed across its releases (153 said yes, 156 says no); a Firefox that says yes gets its default scrollbar, which is acceptable, and C-50 watches it.
- **Text selection.** `::selection { background: var(--prime-soft); color: var(--n-primary); }`
- **Border widths.** 1px everywhere; 2px reserved for meaning: the focus ring, the agent violet border, bucket accents, the tab underline, the drop target, the walkthrough highlight, the avatar-group ring, the stream caret and a chart's bar divider (§9).

### design-spec §6

## 6. Motion & tactility

```css
--t-fast: 120ms; --t-med: 200ms; --t-slow: 320ms; --ease: cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.3,1);
/* the named long durations — decorative, one each */
--t-sweep: 700ms;     /* the primary's hover sweep (§8.1) */
--t-shimmer: 600ms;   /* the meter's one-time shimmer (§8.23) */
--t-skeleton: 1.2s;   /* one pass of the skeleton highlight */
--t-spin: 800ms;      /* one turn of the spinner (§8.17) */
/* delays and holds */
--delay-tooltip: 500ms; /* hover to tooltip (§8.14) */
--delay-working: 300ms; /* send to working line (§8.34) */
--hold-lift: 300ms;     /* touch hold to lift a drag handle (§8.28) */
```

Every duration the stylesheet uses is one of the tokens above. The timers that live in script rather than CSS — the 1.5s validation pause (§8.2), the 1.5s copy tick (§8.16), the 5s and 8s toast clocks (§8.20), the 60s resend cooldown (§8.4), the 0.5 px/ms sheet dismiss velocity (§8.21) — live in one constants module, `motion.ts`, that C-05 reads beside `globals.css`; no component carries a number of its own.

- **Which duration.** Hover and overlay fades: `--t-fast`. Press release, panels, drawers, sheets, modals and toasts: `--t-med`. Meter width: `--t-slow`. Anything not given a curve eases on `--ease`.
- **Responsiveness budget.** A control answers in the frame it is pressed in — the press physics are 0ms in for this reason — and a panel is showing, with focus placed, on the next frame, its morph running from there. A route change paints its chrome and the content's skeleton within 100ms of the click and its content within 1s; past 1s the skeleton stays until the request answers, when content or §10's error line replaces it — never a spinner page. Measured as Interaction to Next Paint on the kitchen-sink route in the browser check, the 75th percentile is at or under 200ms — web.dev's "good" line; C-45 says how the browser check drives and reads it. Motion that would cost a frame is cut, not slowed: this is why blur is rationed (§5), why layout never animates, and why the morph opts the root out. The PS5's interface is described first as fast; nothing else it does would survive being slow.
- **Every exit runs one step faster than its entrance, on `--ease`:** a `--t-slow` entrance leaves over `--t-med`, a `--t-med` entrance over `--t-fast` — the morphed panel excepted, which leaves in one frame (Morph, below). Exits never spring. A top-layer element leaves the top layer the moment it is hidden, so the code applies the leaving state, waits for `transitionend`, then hides — the `overlay` property that would hold it there is not in the baseline, and neither is transitioning `display` with `allow-discrete`, which Firefox does not do.
- **Entrances.** A modal fades in with its scrim and rises 8 over `--t-med`; a toast does the same. A bottom sheet rises its own height, and a side sheet or the overlay drawer slides its own width, over `--t-med`. Entrances use `@starting-style`.
- **Interruptibility.** Every open and close except the morph is a CSS transition on `transform` and `opacity`, so a reversal mid-flight starts from where the surface is. The morph cannot reverse: closing during it ends it at once — every close path, the popover's `toggle` to closed included, calls `skipTransition()` on a running morph.
- **No layout animates.** The docked dock opens and closes in one frame — animating the content column would re-wrap every row each frame and flip rows between 56 and 72 mid-flight (§8.27). The tab underline moves in one frame and tab labels fade colour over `--t-fast` (§8.19). Lists never stagger and rows never animate into a new order: data arrives whole. Follow-scroll, scroll-into-view and composer growth are instant — no programmatic scroll is ever smoothed. Content replaces its skeleton in place (§10). Route changes are instant (§8.3 — the sidebar and the dock persist across them and carry the continuity).
- **Press physics (the retro click — applies to all buttons, chips-with-actions, toggle, checkbox):** on `:active`, instantly (0ms in): `transform: translateY(1px) scale(0.985)`, edge highlight off, `box-shadow: var(--press-shadow)`. On release: spring back over `--t-med` with `--ease-spring`, which overshoots the rest position by a tenth of the travel before settling — the curve peaks at 1.10, and at 1px of travel a tenth of it is a tenth of a pixel, which is why the spring is safe here and nowhere larger. The press must feel immediate; only the release animates, and a re-press during the release starts from wherever the control is. **The gloss press darkens rather than lightens:** `color-mix(in srgb, var(--bg-base) 12%, transparent)` replaces `--press-overlay` on the primary, because a white overlay on a lit surface reads as a highlight, not a press.
- **Morph (the control becomes the surface).** When a trigger opens a menu, select, combobox, filter chip or popover, the panel does not appear beside it: the trigger's box grows into the panel's box, one material throughout, the corner radius interpolating from half the trigger's height to `--r-panel`. Open over `--t-med` on `--ease` — `--ease-spring` belongs to the press, where a tenth of the travel is a tenth of a pixel; on a select growing from 48 to 320 the same overshoot would bounce the panel's bottom edge by 27, which §0 law 1 forbids. **Close is instant on every path** — light dismiss, Esc, Tab and selection alike: the panel leaves in one frame and the trigger returns. The platform hides a light-dismissed popover before any code can wrap it (`beforetoggle` cannot be cancelled on close), so a close morph would run on some paths and not on others, and one close path is worth more than a return flow. **The container morphs; the contents fade.** The transition name sits on the panel's material layer only; the contents carry a second name and fade in over the last `--t-fast` of the open. Naming the contents warps them — a 34 pill becoming a 240×200 panel stretches every glyph between — and text is the worst case. The trigger is `visibility: hidden` for the panel's lifetime, its box reserved, and returns with focus on close.
  **Mechanism, so nobody re-derives it.** The panel is a `popover` — top layer, light dismiss and `Esc` for free, nested popovers closing innermost-first, which is §4's last-opened-wins. Placement is CSS anchor positioning inside `@supports (anchor-name: --a)` — `anchor-name`, an explicit `position-anchor` and `anchor()` only, never `position-visibility`, whose values differ between engines — with a JS positioner writing `top`/`left` from the trigger's rect on open, scroll and resize where it is not supported; the two must be pixel-identical. **Placement rule:** a panel grows downward, and toward the side with more room, unless its own height does not fit below the trigger's top edge; then it grows upward. If it fits neither way it opens toward the larger side with max-height equal to that room less 8. The choice is made at open and held until close. The morph is a same-document View Transition on open only: `document.startViewTransition` wraps the `showPopover()` call; the trigger carries `view-transition-name: morph` in the old state and the panel's material layer carries it in the new. **The image-pair is the material.** `:root::view-transition-group(morph)` keeps the engine's own size-and-position animation and is given only `animation-duration: var(--t-med)`, `animation-timing-function: var(--ease)` and `backdrop-filter: none`; `:root::view-transition-image-pair(morph)`, which fills the group, carries the glass recipe with `box-sizing: border-box`, `--shadow-float`, and a `border-radius` keyframe from `--morph-r0` (half the trigger's height, set inline at open) to `--r-panel` on the same duration and curve with `animation-fill-mode: forwards`; `:root::view-transition-old(morph)` and `:root::view-transition-new(morph)` are `display: none`, so nothing is stretched and the border stays 1px throughout; `:root::view-transition-new(morph-content)` fades in on the delay above. Nothing is ever animated on the group: an author animation there replaces the engine's, and the box would jump to its final size on the first frame while only its corners moved. The blur sits on the image-pair because that is where the radius is — on the group it would blur a square box behind round corners — and the group is told `none` so the engine does not copy the panel's blur onto it as well. Every selector on these pseudo-elements starts at `:root` with no combinator before it, since they hang off the root element. The root opts out (`:root { view-transition-name: none }`) and `:root::view-transition` takes `pointer-events: none`, so the page beneath keeps rendering — a streaming transcript never stalls for a menu — and keeps taking clicks. One transition runs per document: a second open skips the first to its end state, and nothing else in the product calls `startViewTransition`. Names are set at open and cleared at `finished`, never left on elements between transitions — two live elements sharing a name abort every transition on the page. **Focus is placed by the code inside the update callback, straight after `showPopover()`** — on the panel's first item, which in a select is the selected option and in a combobox the input — never left to the transition and never deferred to `finished`, because the hidden trigger loses focus at the next rendering update (the focus-fixup rule) and a keystroke in the gap would go nowhere; the new snapshot carries the ring from its first frame and it fades in with the contents. Where `startViewTransition` is absent, or under `prefers-reduced-motion`, the callback runs bare and the panel simply appears: v2.17's behaviour, so the morph is pure enhancement and can never be the reason a menu fails to open. Element-scoped view transitions would do the root opt-out natively; they are in one engine today, so the document does not name them — revisit when they reach the baseline.
  **What morphs and what does not.** Overflow menus, selects, comboboxes, filter chips, the hand-chrome nav menu, the avatar group and any popover morph from their trigger, anchored at the trigger's nearest corner and placed by the rule above. The chat dock and side sheets slide from their edge over `--t-med`; modals and bottom sheets rise behind a scrim; tooltips have a delay and no origin; disclosures open in the flow of the page. Each already has an origin, and a second one would say nothing. Any open panel closes, and reopens only by hand, when the viewport's *width* changes or the orientation does — its anchor may have moved — and when the docked dock opens or closes, which moves every anchor in the content column without a viewport change; a height-only change (a collapsing browser toolbar, the software keyboard) leaves it open and re-places it.
- **Focus-visible** (keyboard only): `outline: 2px solid var(--prime); outline-offset: 2px;` plus `box-shadow: var(--prime-glow)` — the aero glow lives on focus and on live dots, nowhere else. Pointer focus is not keyboard focus: clicking into a field swaps its border to `--prime` and nothing more. Ring and glow are keyboard affordances. The mechanism is input-modality tracking, not `:focus-visible` — browsers deliberately match `:focus-visible` on a clicked text input, since it is about to take keystrokes, so gating the ring on it reproduces the double stroke this rule removes. Modality is recorded on `<html>` by an inlined script and **starts unset**: a field the page focused already carries a caret, which is the affordance, so ringing it as well reproduces on load the same double stroke this rule removes from the mouse path. The ring arrives on the first Tab or focus key — the first moment there is anything true to say about which device is in use. `:focus-visible` remains correct for buttons and links, where a click does not match it.
- Meter fill animates width `--t-slow`; it animates only a change it witnesses, never from zero on load. Reaching handover-ready (§8.23) triggers a one-time `--t-shimmer` `--hero-gradient` shimmer sweep, once per page open.
- Skeleton shimmer: `--surface-2` base, moving highlight `--hover-overlay`, `--t-skeleton` linear loop. Geometry: a 200%-wide linear-gradient (transparent → highlight → transparent) traversing the element left to right, one pass per loop.
- Small motions, so none is invented: the stepper's connector fills in `--prime` over `--t-med` (§8.31) · a drag lift takes `--t-fast` in and settles over `--t-med` on `--ease-spring` (§8.28) · a set-aside card collapses by animating `grid-template-rows` from `1fr` to `0fr` over `--t-fast`, since height cannot transition to `auto` in every engine (§8.34) · the primary's sweep never runs on a disabled or loading primary (§8.1).
- `prefers-reduced-motion`: kill shimmer, press-translate and press-squish and the primary's hover sweep (keep the inner-shadow state change — it alone carries the press), meter animation, lift, the floating-label slide (§8.2 — the label snaps between positions), the morph (the panel appears in place) and every slide: drawers, sheets, modals and toasts appear in place, a released sheet returns without the spring, and the set-aside card collapses at once. Opacity fades, colour changes and the spinner remain.

### 8.32 Cards

Per §5. **The card's buttons, wherever a card carries moves:** confirm is Primary sm ("Looks right"); set aside, decide later and redirect are Ghost sm; undo lives in the toast, never on the card. **Agent-proposal card:** `--agent-soft` tint + 2px `--agent` left border + confirm; on confirm, violet drops and the card settles to a plain `--surface-1` card (`--t-med` transition — the moment of "human accepted" is visible). **Triage card:** an agent-proposal card — `--agent-soft` tint, 2px `--agent` left border — since its destination is the agent's proposal (§0 law 4): the source icon (a brand glyph, §8.11), the extract quote in ui-body, the proposed destination and confirm/redirect; the violet drops on confirm or redirect, as on any proposal. **Gap card** — a gap drawn on its own, in the transcript or on the item page: a plain card, padding per §5 by where it stands, the check's wording ui-body, its gap chip (§8.9), the quoted evidence on a `--surface-1` card in ui-body, and §8.10's moves at the foot; once accepted or excluded it carries the settled stamp and dims per §2 — text to `--n-secondary`, the non-text parts at .60 — and its Reopen stays fully live. **Time estimate:** a gap card and a walkthrough card carry one ui-footnote `--n-secondary` line beneath the wording, "Typically {n} min" (§12), the number in mono-readout, where product-spec has a typical time for the move; it is small information, never a headline — product-spec's own ruling — and it never renders without a number. The PS5's Activity cards carry a playtime estimate for the same reason: knowing a move takes ten minutes is what makes a person start it. **A card never truncates the text that says what it is for:** its title wraps to two lines before §3's truncation reaches it, and its wording is prose and never truncates; the PS5's cards, which cut trophy names mid-word, are the counter-example.

### design-spec §8.34

### 8.34 Agent states

Working, streaming, settling, pending. Before the first token the agent's turn is a **working line**: a 16 Spinner in `--agent` beside a mono-readout `--n-secondary` phrase naming the step in lowercase — "reading the PRD", "running checks", "drafting" — appearing after `--delay-working` so a fast reply never flashes it, standing until the first token replaces it. It never times out into an apology; if the provider fails, the line becomes the §10 error line, "Couldn't reach the model — retry", with its Secondary sm Retry.
**Streaming.** Text appends in place as it arrives, in the message's own grammar — ui-body on Base for the agent's speech, the violet proposal card for anything that will become content — behind a steady **caret**: a 2×18 `--agent` bar after the last glyph, removed with the final token. It does not blink; §0 law 1 is absolute, and the caret's presence rather than its rhythm says "still writing". Formatting renders as it arrives, and an unclosed code fence renders as mono-code from its opening line rather than waiting to close. The transcript **follows the stream only while the reader is at the bottom** — within 24 of the transcript's end; once they have scrolled up it holds, and a Soft sm "Jump to latest" chip with an `arrow-down` floats bottom-centre of the dock, 16 above the composer, until it is tapped or the bottom is reached; the follow itself is instant (§6). A stream can be stopped: the composer's send IconButton becomes the `square` stop for the stream's duration (§8.33), and a stopped reply keeps what arrived with a mono-readout "stopped" trailing it.
**Settling.** A proposal card the person confirms drops its violet over `--t-med` (§8.32); one they set aside collapses over `--t-fast` (§6 — `grid-template-rows` to `0fr`) and leaves a ui-footnote `--n-secondary` line "Set aside" in its place for the rest of the session, so the transcript never has a hole where a decision was.
**Optimistic or pending.** Human moves split by reversibility. *Optimistic* — park, confirm, accept, reopen, decide-later: the interface applies the change at once and offers the undo toast; on failure it reverts, dismisses that move's undo at once and the toast says what did not happen ("Couldn't park — try again"), leading dot `--warning`, never red. Every optimistic move has a standing reversal on the page besides the toast (§8.20). *Pending* — anything irreversible or external: signing a packet, sending a code, any write that leaves aenima takes the button's loading state (spinner replaces label, width locked) and applies only on the server's answer; nothing shows as done before it is. Agent-initiated external writes always pass through a confirm first (§0 law 4), so the agent has no optimistic state of its own.

### design-spec §11

## 11. Keyboard & focus

`Cmd/Ctrl+K` opens the chat if it is closed and focuses the composer; pressed while the composer already has focus it closes the drawer, or in the docked modes returns focus to where it came from (§8.33) · `Esc` closes the most recently opened layer (last-opened-wins, per §4 — not the highest rung) and never changes the route · `Enter` on a proposal's confirm button confirms it — the card's buttons are its Tab stops, and a card is never focusable itself — and `Cmd/Ctrl+Enter` sends in the composer · `Cmd/Ctrl+Z` triggers a showing undo toast when focus is not in a text field (§8.20) · arrow keys walk menus/selects/list rows · `/` focuses search in list views · `?` opens the shortcut sheet (§8.38) · a focused row's overflow menu is reached with Right (the grid model below) — the `ContextMenu` key and `Shift+F10` stay the browser's, as right-click does (§7, §16). **Global shortcuts work regardless of what has focus** — the reason a console gives the guide its own button — with two exceptions: the unmodified keys (`/`, `?`) and `Cmd/Ctrl+Z` are inert while focus is in a text field, where they are typing; and while a modal or sheet is open only Esc and the layer's own keys act, since a shortcut that moved focus out of the trap would land in the inert page. `Cmd/Ctrl+K` acts everywhere else; `Cmd/Ctrl+Enter` belongs to the composer alone. **Focus starts in the content.** On a fresh load the first Tab stop is a "Skip to content" link — a Neutral sm pill (not Soft: in hand chrome it stands over the top bar's glass, the toast's case, §8.20), visible only while focused, at the top-left of the viewport inset 16 on both axes, rung 600 — ahead of the sidebar's seven stops (lockup, switcher, four nav items, account), or in hand chrome the top bar's four (mark, switcher, chat, menu). After a route change the code focuses the main region (`tabindex="-1"`), so the next Tab lands on the page's first control and a screen reader announces the page by its title; **the main region never draws the ring, whatever the modality** — it is a landmark, not a control, and the ring is a control's affordance — the one exemption from §6's rule and C-31. A console chooses where focus starts so that reaching anything costs the fewest presses; the web's default of starting at the top of the document is the opposite choice. **The way back restores the place.** Returning to a list — by Back, by the breadcrumb, by the sidebar — restores its scroll position and, instead of the main region, focuses the row that was opened (with the ring under keyboard modality, since a row is a control); a filter or page in the URL (§8.27, §8.29) restores with it; the dock keeps its scroll and its remembered control (§4). Panels and sheets never remember: they reopen fresh. **The Esc stack** holds, in opening order: panels, modals and sheets, the overlay drawer, a live selection. A showing tooltip takes Esc first and closes nothing else; an active drag takes it before the stack — Esc cancels the drag and closes nothing. Toasts are not in it; a toast never takes focus on arrival — `Cmd/Ctrl+Z` is the keyboard path to its undo, and Tab reaches it last in document order. **Menus, selects and tabs carry roving focus:** one Tab stop; arrows move within and wrap; Home and End jump; a typed letter jumps to the next match; Enter or Space activates; Esc closes a panel and returns focus to its trigger; Tab closes the panel and carries focus to the stop after the trigger, Shift+Tab to the one before — in a select, committing the active option first. **A list is one Tab stop and a grid inside:** Up and Down move between rows; Right and Left walk the focused row's controls in the one-line row's visual order, at both row heights — handle, checkbox, name, chip, on an idle row the Park chip, overflow trigger; Enter opens the item from the name; Home and End jump; in a selectable list Space toggles the focused row, `Shift`+Up/Down extend the selection, `Cmd/Ctrl+A` takes every rendered row. On a drag handle Space grabs, arrows move, Space drops, Esc cancels. Every interactive element reachable by keyboard in visual order; focus ring per §6; focus trapped inside modals and sheets; on close, focus returns to the opener — after a morph, the trigger the panel grew from.
