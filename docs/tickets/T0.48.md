## Objective

The app's frame follows design-spec §4: four layout modes — wide, standard, desk and hand — switching
at 1440, 1280 and 1024; the top bar that replaces the sidebar below 1024, its menu morphing into the
nav; the sidebar's and the page topbar's geometry; the read-only line, 768 on touch and 600 on
pointer, with every write marked `data-writes` and absent below it; the 768 component line, where
modals and side sheets become bottom sheets and toasts, tabs and the pipeline strip take their narrow
form; chrome that renders before data; the 200% zoom rule; and the responsiveness budget, measured on
the kitchen-sink route. Where the chat stands in each mode is the dock's — T3.2 split it to *Build the
dock surface* at Backlog — and this ticket builds the frame it stands in.

## Rules

As the repository's: `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, `docs/guidelines.md`. Every value comes from
design-spec (v2.23 as read); one the build needs and cannot find there is a Decision, never a pick.
Every check here is *browser* — it runs in a real engine, never the DOM emulator — C-25's grep half
aside.

- The mode is viewport width alone; touch and pointer are `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` and
  nothing else (§7). A 375 pointer window is hand chrome on pointer rules and read-only below 600, not
  768; a 1024 iPad in landscape is the sidebar on touch rules.
- Below the read-only line a write is absent from the DOM, never disabled or hidden.
- Nothing in chrome skeletons but the topbar's title and freshness slots; nothing in chrome is
  disabled while content loads.
- `globals.css` declares no custom property on `:root` beyond the document's blocks and the z-ladder
  (`src/app/tokens.test.ts`, C-05); no component stylesheet carries a literal colour, radius,
  duration, shadow or z-index (C-06, `scripts/design/laws.mjs`) — a px breakpoint is none of those.
- Blur only on sticky bars, panels and toasts (C-37): the top bar and the sticky page topbar take the
  blurred class; bottom sheets and the strip the unblurred one.
- No route is invented: `ROUTES` holds every URL, a new one is a public surface, and Graveyard has none
  until its ticket. Nothing is written to `docs/tickets/T3.2.md` or any earlier ticket's files.
- Server Components by default: the frame's client islands are the top bar's menu, the switcher, the
  account menu and the write gate; the mode itself is CSS.

## 4. Layout, z-order, chrome

- **Grid.** Sidebar 240 · content column max 1200, centred, gutters 24 (16 in hand chrome) · chat dock 380 · item page = content 1fr / chat 380 while the dock is docked. Root 16px and breakpoints in CSS px, so browser zoom degrades into the narrower mode rather than breaking the wider one. **A mode's content column includes its gutters; the content box is the column less two gutters, and every container rule measures the content box** — the row's line count (§8.27) among them.
- **Layout modes.** Four, decided by viewport width alone. Touch and pointer are decided separately (§7), so a narrow desktop window gets hand chrome with pointer rules, an 11-inch iPad in portrait gets hand chrome with touch rules, and a 13-inch iPad in landscape gets the sidebar with touch rules.

| Mode | Width | Chrome | Content column | Chat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wide | ≥1440 | sidebar | min(1200, vw − 620): 820 at 1440; 1200 with the dock closed; beyond 1820 the surplus is margin, split evenly around the column | docked, open by default |
| Standard | 1280–1439 | sidebar | vw − 240, less 380 while the dock is open: 660–819 open, 1040–1199 closed | docked; closed by default on lists, open by default on the item page |
| Desk | 1024–1279 | sidebar | vw − 240: 784–1039 | overlay drawer |
| Hand | <1024 | top bar | vw | overlay drawer down to the read-only line; the read-only full-height bottom sheet below it |

  The arithmetic is the reason for the cut-offs. A docked dock beside a 240 sidebar leaves 660 at 1280 and 404 at 1024: 660 holds the item page and the two-line row (§8.27), 404 holds nothing, so the dock docks at 1280 and floats below it. A 240 sidebar beside content at 768 leaves 528, which fits nothing on the row, so the sidebar yields to a top bar below 1024. **Below the read-only line the product is read-only** — the line is 768 on touch and 600 on pointer, because a 200% zoom on a 1280 laptop is 640 wide and is not a phone (§13) — every mutation absent from the page, not disabled (the list is under Top bar) — **except the auth flow**, which must be fully usable at 375: an invited member signs in on whatever they are holding, and any control on the path from invite link to landed session carries a <768 rule. Walkthrough answers and signatures are the first v1.1 mobile writes (§16). **What a mutation is:** a control that writes carries `data-writes`, and below the read-only line no component renders one outside the auth path — gap moves, park, confirm, redirect, decide later, the walkthrough answer, the drag handle, bulk selection, the sign block, the composer and every settings form. Sign out, Retry, Copy, filters, paging, Show more, the product switcher and Jump to latest do not write and remain; C-25 asserts on the attribute.
- **The 768 line.** A component breakpoint on the viewport, separate from the read-only line: below it modals and side sheets are bottom sheets (§8.21), the OTP steps down (§8.2), toasts span the width (§8.20), tabs, the pipeline strip and tables scroll rather than wrap (§8.19, §8.26, §8.22), the stepper drops its labels (§8.31) and a chart takes the full width (§9). It applies at the same width on pointer and touch. It equals the touch read-only line because phones are narrow, not by rule, so on pointer between 600 and 767 these components take their narrow form and keep their writes.
- **Height.** The supported floor is 600 tall; below it nothing is hidden, the page scrolls. Sticky bars keep their height at every height. Modals and bottom sheets are at most 90% of the viewport tall — the full-height transcript sheet of §8.21 excepted — and a side sheet is the full height; all three scroll inside themselves and keep their footer or header pinned. The auth step keeps the Æ mark and drops its top spacing to 16 below 600 tall, so a landscape phone still sees a step whole. The dock's transcript scrolls inside the dock and its composer is pinned.
- **Density.** List rows 56 in a content box of 760 or more, 72 as two lines below it (§8.27) · table rows 44 · menu and option rows 36 on pointer, 44 on touch · hit areas ≥40 on pointer devices, ≥44 on touch, menu and option rows excepted on pointer (§7 Hit areas).
- **Sidebar (wide, standard, desk).** `--bg-base`; **a column of three zones — lockup, nav, account.** Lockup top: Æ mark 24px + `aenima` wordmark, one link to the dashboard; product switcher directly beneath it = avatar 40 + display-md, chevron `nav-arrow-down` 20, a menu trigger (§8.18) whose rows are the products; nav items 40 on pointer and 44 on touch (icon 20 + ui-body) — flush rows cannot extend a hit area, so on touch they grow for real, as menu rows do (§7 Hit areas) — active = `--prime-soft` pill + `--n-primary`. **Geometry:** the sidebar pads 16 inline; the lockup and the switcher are 56 rows, the bar height; the nav begins 16 below the switcher, its items flush with 12 inline padding and 8 from icon to label (§8.2's gap); the account slot is a 56 row. The nav's four glyphs, from Iconoir (§8.11): Dashboard `dashboard-dots` · Triage `mail-in` · Graveyard `archive` · Settings `settings`; Sign out is `log-out` wherever it appears. **The account slot is pinned to the bottom:** avatar 32 + the signed-in address in ui-body, truncating; it is a menu trigger (§8.18) whose rows are "Keyboard shortcuts" (`key-command`, §8.38 — always on pointer devices, and on touch once the modality script has recorded keyboard input, §8.15) and "Sign out" (`log-out`). Sign out is a plain row and takes no confirm: ending a session destroys nothing that was saved, and a composer draft is a draft. Identity is something you check and change, not something you navigate between, and the top-left position belongs to the things that move you around — the switcher stays at the top because it filters the list rather than identifying the person. There is no collapse control on the sidebar; below 1024 the top bar replaces it, which is a breakpoint rather than a control (§16). The chat dock is what collapses.
- **Top bar (hand).** 56h plus `env(safe-area-inset-top)`, sticky, glass recipe with `--scrim-top`: the Æ mark 24 at the left, one link to the dashboard as in the sidebar's lockup, the product switcher beside it (avatar 32, name display-md, truncating), and at the right a `chat-bubble` IconButton and a **menu IconButton** (`menu`), both Neutral 34, the menu one morphing (§6) into the nav menu — Dashboard, Triage, Graveyard, Settings, a separator, the account row — static, not a `menuitem`, standing where a section title would (avatar 24, address truncating) — then "Keyboard shortcuts" (on pointer devices, and on touch once keyboard input has been recorded, §8.15) and Sign out beneath it. One mechanism, no bottom tab bar. The page title steps down to display-lg; the topbar's primary action becomes an IconButton when it has an icon and a sm button when it does not. Mechanics: `viewport-fit=cover` with safe-area insets on every sticky bar, sheet and toast; no input font below 16 at any width, so Safari never zooms a field; `touch-action: manipulation` on controls; `-webkit-tap-highlight-color: transparent`, since press physics are the tap feedback; a focused field scrolls into view above the software keyboard. **Below the read-only line the mutations are absent:** every `data-writes` control — gap moves, park, confirm, overflow menus, bulk selection, the sign block and the composer among them — is not rendered, so nothing invites a tap it will refuse.
- **Chrome renders before data.** The sidebar, the top bar, the page topbar and the dock are drawn from the route alone, before any request returns, and chrome is never disabled while content loads — the person can always navigate, search and open the chat, whatever the page is still fetching. The two data slots inside chrome — the page topbar's title where it is an item's name, and its freshness readout — carry a skeleton of their own size until their data arrives, and the dock's transcript is content and loads per §10; nothing else in chrome ever skeletons. This is the PlayStation 5's one unarguable lesson: its Control Center answers a single press at once, over a game that is still loading, and that immediacy is most of what the interface is praised for.
- **Chat placement.** The dock is aenima's Control Center: one key from anywhere (`Cmd/Ctrl+K`), over the work rather than instead of it, the work still running underneath, and it puts the person back where they were when it closes. *Docked* (wide, standard): a 380 column at the right edge that pushes the content column, opening and closing in one frame (§6 — animating the column would re-wrap every row each frame). It closes only by the page topbar's `chat-bubble` IconButton: `Cmd/Ctrl+K` opens it and never closes it, and it is not in the Esc stack (§11). *Overlay drawer* (desk, and hand down to the read-only line): 380 wide from the right edge at rung 200, sliding its own width over `--t-med` and leaving over `--t-fast`, with no scrim — clicks on the content behind it go through, since the point of a drawer over a document is to read both — opened by the `chat-bubble` IconButton in the page topbar or the hand top bar, or by `Cmd/Ctrl+K`; closed by its own close IconButton (`xmark`), Esc, or `Cmd/Ctrl+K` from the composer. As an overlay the dock keeps `--bg-base` — a transcript is content, and §0 law 10 keeps glass off content — and takes `--shadow-modal`, since it now floats. *Bottom sheet* (below the read-only line): the full-height sheet of §8.21, read-only, with the subtitle "Reply from a desktop" standing where the composer would be. **Defaults and memory:** the dock is open by default everywhere in wide mode; in standard mode closed by default on lists and open by default on the item page, because the chat is the work there; closed everywhere in desk and hand. The defaults apply until the person toggles the dock, and from then the choice holds for the session, separately for list pages and the item page. The dock survives route changes — transcript, draft, scroll position and a stream in flight carry across — and remembers which of its controls had focus when it closed: reopening by the toggle lands focus there, or on the composer when nothing is remembered; `Cmd/Ctrl+K` always lands on the composer where there is one (§8.33), because the key is the command palette. (The PS5's Switcher, which closes what it claims to switch between, is the failure this rule exists to avoid.)
- **Topbar per page.** 56 tall — the hand bar's and the selection bar's height — growing by 30 when it carries a subtitle, inline padding the mode's gutter: display-xl title (display-lg in hand chrome) + mono-readout freshness + at most one primary action, and in the sidebar modes a `chat-bubble` IconButton (Neutral 34, `aria-pressed` while the dock is open) at the trailing edge after the primary. In the sidebar modes the topbar is sticky, glass recipe + `--scrim-top`; in hand chrome only the app top bar sticks and the page topbar scrolls with the page — two stacked sticky bars would take a fifth of a 600-tall screen. **A sticky bar's scrim is its own non-interactive layer directly behind the bar,** the bar's width, running from the bar's outer edge to three-sevenths of the bar's height beyond its content-side edge — `calc(<bar height> * 3 / 7)`, 24 beyond a 56 bar, and the same fraction of their own height for the topbar with a subtitle and for the hand top bar with its safe-area inset — so the scrim's alpha where content meets the bar is 0.144 on every bar: content dims as it approaches the bar and as it passes under the glass, a margin on top of the §13 ratio the glass fill already holds.
- **Subtitle slot.** Any page or step title may carry one subtitle line beneath it: ui-body, `--n-secondary`, 8 below the title. Instructional copy ("We'll send a six-digit code") lives here, and only here — never in a field's helper line (§8.2). One line; it truncates rather than wraps on narrow widths — except where the subtitle carries an address the person must check: the OTP subtitle wraps to as many lines as the address needs and never truncates it, since touch has no tooltip and no page holds that string as content.
- **Z-ladder** (never improvise): content 0 · sticky bars 100 · chat dock 200 · dropdown/popover 300 · modal scrim+modal 400 · toast 500 · tooltip and the skip link (§11) 600. **The ladder governs painting only.** Dismissal is last-opened-wins: `Esc` closes the most recently opened layer regardless of its rung, so a select opened inside a modal takes the first `Esc` and the modal takes the second. Ranking dismissal by rung would strand the inner layer. **The ladder governs what is not in the browser's top layer.** Popovers and dialogs paint in the top layer in the order they opened, above every rung — which puts a menu above the modal it was opened from, the same answer the ladder gives. Toasts and tooltips are `popover="manual"` so they too live there. Top-layer order is opening order and a modal makes everything outside its own subtree inert, so a toast is not left to that order: **the toast host is a `popover="manual"` that is moved into the topmost open modal's subtree — as its last child, and otherwise the last child of `body` — and re-shown whenever a modal opens or closes,** so it paints above the scrim, stays operable and is last in document order for Tab; its clock carries over the move. A tooltip is created in the same place — inside the topmost open modal's subtree, otherwise in `body` — so one raised over a modal's control can be hovered (§8.14). The rung numbers remain the tie-break for everything outside the top layer — sticky bars, the selection bar, the dock.
- **Overlay interior.** Modals, sheets, and toasts pad 20; stacked content inside them gaps on the 8-grid. Panels (menu, select, combobox, popover) originate on their trigger and carry no gap — the trigger sits inside the panel's footprint for as long as it is open (§6 Morph).
- **Scrollbars.** 8px thin; thumb `--surface-2`, hover `--glass-border`; track transparent. Mechanism: `scrollbar-color` and `scrollbar-width: thin` inside `@supports not selector(::-webkit-scrollbar)`, and the 8px thumb with its `--glass-border` hover through `::-webkit-scrollbar` elsewhere — an engine that honours the standard properties discards every `::-webkit-scrollbar` rule on the same element, so the two must never meet. Firefox gets no hover colour. The gate is Firefox's answer to `selector(::-webkit-scrollbar)`, which has changed across its releases (153 said yes, 156 says no); a Firefox that says yes gets its default scrollbar, which is acceptable, and C-50 watches it.
- **Text selection.** `::selection { background: var(--prime-soft); color: var(--n-primary); }`
- **Border widths.** 1px everywhere; 2px reserved for meaning: the focus ring, the agent violet border, bucket accents, the tab underline, the drop target, the walkthrough highlight, the avatar-group ring, the stream caret and a chart's bar divider (§9).

### 8.21 Modals & sheets

Scrim `--bg-scrim`. Modal: glass recipe without blur (§5), `--r-md`, `--shadow-modal`, max 400 (confirm) / 640 (content); title display-lg; entering as §6 says. **Footers:** every modal carries one, at least a Neutral md "Close" (§8.38's grammar), so the scrim is never its only way out; md buttons, gap 8, right-aligned, primary last; in a bottom sheet lg, full width, stacked, primary last. Side sheets (evidence, decision log): 480 wide, the full viewport height at the right edge, sliding in its own width over `--t-med` and out over `--t-fast`, glass recipe without blur, `--r-lg` on the leading corners only. A side sheet carries no footer and instead a 56 header — title display-md, a Neutral 34 close IconButton (`xmark`) at the right, as in the full-height sheet's header below — at every width, for the same reason. **A side sheet is modal:** it carries the scrim, traps focus and closes on a scrim click — it is the overlay drawer (§4) that has no scrim, because a drawer over a document is for reading both. A modal is at most 90% of the viewport tall (§4 Height); both scroll inside themselves and keep their footer or header pinned, and a pinned footer or header carries no material of its own (§5). At 768–1023 a modal keeps its maxima, centred, and a side sheet keeps its 480. **Below 768 a modal and a side sheet are both bottom sheets:** glass recipe without blur, `--r-lg` on the top corners only, rising from the bottom edge over `--t-med`, a grabber — 36×4, `--n-disabled`, `--r-pill`, 8 from the top — above the content, `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)` padded, the page behind never scrolling. Dismiss by the scrim, by Esc, or by the grabber: **the sheet follows the finger 1:1 downward and never above rest; release dismisses when the drag has passed 80 *or* the release velocity exceeds 0.5 px/ms downward** (a quick short flick is a dismissal to anyone who has used a sheet), the exit continuing from the finger's position over `--t-fast`; short of both it returns over `--t-med` on `--ease-spring`, its fill extending 8 below the viewport so the overshoot opens no seam. The velocity lives in `motion.ts` (§6). On pointer the grabber is not drawn and the sheet does not follow a drag — §7 gives pointer no swipe, and a grabber there would invite a drag it refuses (§4); the sheet closes by its footer or header, the scrim or Esc. **A full-height sheet** (the read-only transcript) drops the radius and keeps a 56 header — title display-md, a Neutral 34 close IconButton (`xmark`) at the right — so a drag is never the only way out. Focus is trapped inside a sheet exactly as inside a modal.

## 10. System states & degraded pages

- **No AI key:** meters render hollow tracks + "Connect AI to activate scoring" (ui-footnote) — never zeros, never red. **That is the item page, where the line stands beside the track and says what the emptiness means.** A list row has no room for the line, so the hollow track there is an unlabelled stub repeated once per row — noise that explains nothing. **Row micro-meters do not render at all without a key**; the space goes to the content, and the meters appear when the scores do.
- **Provider outage / retry:** freshness shows `--warning` dot + mono-readout "scored 6 h ago — retrying" on the item page; a row shows the `--warning` dot alone, its readout unchanged; no banners.
- **404/500/offline:** empty-state pattern on dot grid, Æ mark 32, one line, one action ("Back to dashboard" / "Retry"). 500 may show a mono-readout incident id.
- Full-page loads: skeleton screens mirroring the target layout — the content column only, never the chrome, which renders from the route before any data (§4) — never a centered spinner page. **Content replaces its skeleton in place and fades in over `--t-fast`; the skeleton leaves in the same frame.** Route changes are instant (§6, §8.3).
- **Every data-bound component carries three states of its own, at its own size.** *Loading* is a skeleton of the component's own shape — rows for a list, a track for a meter, a bar for a chart — never a spinner where a list will be. *Empty* is the compact empty state: one ui-body `--n-secondary` line at the component's own padding and at most one action, no icon, no texture — the full §8.37 empty state with its icon and dot grid is for a page, not a card. *Error* is a ui-footnote `--n-secondary` line naming what did not load and a Secondary sm "Retry" — never `--danger`, because a load failure is not the person's error and §0 law 2 reserves red for what is. A page never fails as a whole because one component did.

## 13. Accessibility

- On Base, `--n-primary` 15.7:1 and `--n-secondary` 7.9:1; `--prime` text 8.5:1; `--agent` 7.4:1; `--warning` 9.7:1; `--danger` 7.5:1. Re-measured on the v2.8 ramp, where the deeper base lifts every one of them. Read against the worst surface in the ramp rather than Base, the same six are 11.1 · 5.5 · 6.0 · 5.2 · 6.8 · 5.3 — all clear AA, and the accents clear 5.1. **Placeholder and Disabled fail AA by design** — never information-carrying.
- Accent text/icons always use bright tones; deep tones are fills with labels on them.
- Meters always pair color with the numeric value; gap states carry text labels, not color alone.
- Focus-visible on everything; full keyboard paths (list → item → chat → sign); `prefers-reduced-motion` per §6. **Text on glass is measured on `--glass-fill` over `--prime` with no scrim** — the brightest large fill that can pass under a panel or a toast, which carry no scrim — and C-29 computes it: at the fill's .85, `--n-secondary` holds 4.77:1 there, and a sticky bar's scrim (§4) only adds margin. The fill is .85 rather than .80 because at .80 that stack measures 4.32, under AA.
- Dimming never takes text below AA (§2): idle and parked rows and settled gap cards step their text to `--n-secondary` and dim only their non-text parts.
- Under `prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce` every glass surface takes §5's fallback; under `prefers-contrast: more` it does the same and `--glass-border` becomes `--n-secondary`. The first query matches in one engine today, so both are enhancements and neither gates.
- **Text zoom.** At 200% browser zoom every string stays readable and whole and no control clips: the px breakpoints (§4) carry the layout into the narrower mode, rows take their two lines, and nothing is exempt — the PS5's "larger text" enlarges some of its interface and not the rest, and the reviewers who needed it noticed at once. Zoom is a width change, so the read-only line (§4) is where it bites: a 1280 laptop at 200% is 640 wide, which is why the line is 600 on pointer rather than 768 — a zoomed desktop keeps its writes; a phone does not.
- Floating labels are real `<label>` elements bound to their inputs at every moment of the animation — the at-rest state is a styled label, never a placeholder standing in for one.
- Menus are `role="menu"` with `menuitem` rows; selects and comboboxes are `combobox` with a `listbox`; lists are a `grid` of one Tab stop (§11); the streaming transcript is `aria-live="polite"` with `aria-busy` true for the stream's duration, so a screen reader hears a reply once, whole, rather than every token. Drag is fully keyboard-operable and announces each move. Hit areas are 44 on touch and 40 on pointer — menu and option rows are their own hit areas, exempt from the pointer floor, and everything smaller extends its hit area (§7 Hit areas). A view transition never moves focus on its own: the code places it inside the update callback, on the panel's first item — a select's selected option, a combobox's input (§6).
