## Defaults taken under §4

- **Where a probe lives.** The ticket names probes and not their shape. They sit on the check
  (`RubricCheck.probes`) rather than as a sibling list on the pack the way the interview bank
  does, because a probe is read *under* its check by the scorer and by a person reviewing the
  pack, and a sibling list would need a binding and a lookup to say the same thing. A developer
  shape, cheap to move; said in one `default` comment on the board row.
- **The protocol says what a probe is.** Without a sentence in the run layer the scorer would
  meet indented questions under a check with no rule for them. One paragraph, no rubric content
  — "a probe is not a check" — and it is what moves `PROTOCOL_RELEASE`, which AC2 asks for.
- **`prd-19`'s sweep reaches binding lines, not background prose.** §7.2 row 19 reads "no
  sentence two developers could read two ways", and the probes put the sweep to "each
  requirement, story and acceptance line … not the background prose", so a PRD whose only
  two-way sentence sits in its motivation passes this check under pack 1.1.0. The reading is
  §7.2's own example — check 19 failing *at* `MN-2`, a requirement — and Appendix B item 19,
  whose critic returns two-readings sentences as pointed choices a developer must pick between;
  a background sentence read two ways produces no two implementations, and a sweep that counted
  it would fail nearly every document on its first paragraph. Row 19's wording is unchanged and
  the header of `feature-prd.ts` says so; if the Owner wants the row's full reach, the two
  clauses come out of the third probe set and `prd-19` is re-measured. Raised by the reviewer
  (pass 1, Must); said in the `default` comment on the board row.
- **An empty probe is refused at load.** The ticket names no such rule. `validatePack` already
  refuses a broken budget and an unbound interview question at load, and a probe of whitespace
  would render as a blank question the scorer is told to answer; the same rule, one line.

## Open questions

1. **`prd-15`'s condition still moves — held in 3 of 11, as 4 of 11 in T2.7.** It is §4's
   applicability answer, not a verdict, and the ticket's five are verdicts; the 0.3 points of
   spread left are all of it. A probe on a *condition* would be the same cure, but a condition's
   `when` is carried "in the spec's own words" (§4) and the type has probes on checks only —
   whether a condition may carry probes is a spec question. Owner: T-next, with a spec patch to
   §4 if the answer is yes.
2. **Settled is not the same as right.** All five fail on this document, and the notes read as
   the correct fails (above), but that is one reader's judgement on one document. The golden set
   (build-log *On the horizon*, the scorer eval harness) is what grades it, and it now has a
   repeatable baseline to grade against — the ordering T2.7 argued for. Owner: the eval-harness
   ticket.
3. **The fabrication guard refused one call in twelve, and it was a real paraphrase.** The
   eleventh call's `prd-12` quote — "The other person is notified immediately, nothing settles,
   the chat stays open." — is the document's "The other is notified immediately, nothing
   settles, the chat stays open." with one word added. Not build-log open question 29's corner
   (a quote spanning bold markers): `readAnswer` refused the run rightly, §5's failed run wrote
   nothing, and a twelfth call replaced it. A probe asks the scorer to *name* the unanswered
   question, which may invite composing where the check's own prose did not; one in twelve is a
   rate to watch, not yet a finding. Owner: the eval-harness ticket, which will see the rate
   over many documents.
4. **Every stored meter re-scores.** Both halves of the cache key moved, so the next open of any
   scored item calls the provider again and, on documents like this one, lands lower: this
   document went from a median of 19.2 to 5.1, because the sufficiency checks were most of what
   it was earning. That is §5's re-baseline working as written — "numbers never wobble without
   explanation" — and the explanation is this report. No surface says so to a reader today;
   whether the meter should is Phase 4's. Owner: none needed.
5. **Build-log open question 29 names T2.8 as the ticket to carry a quoting rule into the
   protocol** — "quote whole sentences" or "quote without markdown syntax" — because this
   ticket pays the re-baseline anyway. The ticket body does not list it, and it was found after
   arm P had run: adding a sentence now would move the fingerprint a second time, so either the
   eleven runs above would no longer be runs of the shipped protocol or they would be paid for
   twice. Not carried, said in the build log beside the question. It rides with the next ticket
   that moves `PROTOCOL_VERSION`; open question 23 (a passing check citing the document) is the
   same arithmetic and the same ticket. Owner: T-next.
6. **Five probes are rules, not questions** (reviewer pass 1, Should 2): "one missing fails the
   check", "silence is a gap, an explicit none is an answer". They did their work on this
   document, but the protocol says a probe is answered from the artifact, and a rule cannot be.
   The fix is one sentence in the protocol paragraph — a probe the artifact answers only in part
   is unanswered, and an explicit "none" answers it — and the five rule-probes deleted from the
   pack; both move what the scorer reads, so it rides with open question 5's ticket and is
   measured there. Two things that ticket has to carry with it (reviewer pass 2): the protocol's
   rule is written for *presence* probes, and `prd-19`'s are a *search* — an artifact that
   answers "could two developers read this two ways?" with a clear yes is the failing case, and
   today only the rule-shaped third probe inverts it — so the protocol needs a sentence for
   search-shaped probes (absence answers them; a hit is the gap) or `prd-19`'s probes become
   presence questions; and `prd-16`'s first two probes ask for permission-denied and offline
   behaviour unconditionally while its fourth narrows to "the three conditions this feature can
   meet", two readings of one probe set on a network-dependent PRD that asks no permission —
   fold the condition into the questions ("If the feature asks a permission …") and drop the
   fourth. Owner: T-next.
